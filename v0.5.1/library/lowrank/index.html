The low-rank distribution and the grid factor space

VlasovMethods.AbstractFactorSpace — Type
AbstractFactorSpace{DT}

The supertype of the spaces that hold the factors of a LowRankDistribution, with the element type DT. A concrete space defines size(space) = (nx, nv), the lengths of the x and the v factors, and extends the four functions of its weighted inner products: inner_x, inner_v, orthonormalize_x and orthonormalize_v. eltype(space) is DT.

source
VlasovMethods.GridFactorSpace — Type
GridFactorSpace(x, v)
GridFactorSpace(dist::GridDistribution)

The factor space of the nodes of a GridDistribution: the periodic x grid x and the bounded v grid v, two ranges of one element type DT. x holds at least one node and v at least two. size(space) is (length(x), length(v)).

The inner products are the rectangle rules of the grid, with $h_x =$ step(x) and $h_v =$ step(v):

\[\langle a, b \rangle_x = h_x \sum_i a_i b_i , \qquad \langle a, b \rangle_v = h_v \sum_j a_j b_j ,\]

so the rule in v is the rule of velocity_moments of a GridDistribution. VlasovMethods.inner_x and VlasovMethods.inner_v evaluate them, and VlasovMethods.orthonormalize_x and VlasovMethods.orthonormalize_v orthonormalise in them by the Householder QR of LinearAlgebra.qr.

VlasovMethods.LowRankDistribution — Type
LowRankDistribution{DT, ST}

A distribution function of a 1D1V phase space in the factored form

\[f \approx X S V^\mathsf{T} ,\]

a DistributionFunction{DT, 1, 1}. Its fields:

  • space, the factor space, a concrete subtype of VlasovMethods.AbstractFactorSpace{DT}, with nx, nv = size(space);
  • X, the nx × r Matrix{DT} of the x factors;
  • S, the r × r coefficient Matrix{DT}, so the rank is r = size(S, 1);
  • V, the nv × r Matrix{DT} of the v factors;
  • nfixed, the number of leading columns of V that span the fixed velocity modes.

The type is mutable: a step may assign to X, S, V and nfixed factors of another rank. An assignment of another AbstractMatrix{DT} to X, S or V converts it to a Matrix{DT}. The assignment is not checked against the sizes. size(d) is size(d.space) and eltype(d) is DT.

LowRankDistribution(space, X, S, V; nfixed = 0)

Take the factors X, S and V, of the element type DT of space. A Matrix{DT} factor is stored without a copy; any other AbstractMatrix{DT}, such as a view or a Diagonal, is converted to a new Matrix{DT}. A factor of another element type raises a MethodError. Throws a DimensionMismatch for factors of other sizes, and an ArgumentError for a rank r outside 1:min(nx, nv) or nfixed outside 0:r. It does not check that X and V are orthonormal: that is the caller's contract.

LowRankDistribution(dist::GridDistribution, r; fixed)

Factorise the node values f = dist.values at the rank r on GridFactorSpace(dist). fixed is an nv × m matrix of velocity modes, converted to the element type of dist, none by default. With $h_x$, $h_v$ the steps of the grid:

  1. the fixed modes are orthonormalised first, by the weighted QR of fixed, so the first k columns of V span the first k columns of fixed, and nfixed = m;
  2. the next q columns of V span the leading right singular vectors of $\sqrt{h_x h_v}\, g$, with $g = f - h_v f V_m V_m^\mathsf{T}$ the part of f outside the fixed modes $V_m$, where q is the smaller of r - m and the number of singular values above $\max(n_x, n_v)\, \epsilon \, \| \sqrt{h_x h_v} f \|_F$, with $\epsilon$ = eps(DT);
  3. the columns m + q + 1 to r complete V deterministically, from the full orthogonal factor of the Householder QR of $\sqrt{h_v} [V_m \; V_q]$, with $V_q$ the q singular vectors of item 2, divided by $\sqrt{h_v}$; no random numbers are drawn;
  4. X, S = orthonormalize_x(space, h_v f V).

So $X S V^\mathsf{T} = h_v f V V^\mathsf{T}$ is the weighted orthogonal projection of f onto the span of V, with $h_x X^\mathsf{T} X = I$ and $h_v V^\mathsf{T} V = I$. Without fixed modes it is the weighted truncated SVD of rank r; with m fixed modes it keeps every moment of f that the fixed modes span, such as the density, the momentum and the energy for the modes [1 v v²].

Throws an ArgumentError for r outside 1:min(nx, nv), for m > r, for linearly dependent fixed modes, and for a non-finite entry of f or of fixed; and a DimensionMismatch for fixed of another number of rows than nv.

The factored fields

VlasovMethods.LowRankFields1D1V — Type
LowRankFields1D1V(arakawa::Arakawa{T}, G::AbstractMatrix{T}, v::AbstractVector;
                  closure = PeriodicVelocityClosure())
LowRankFields1D1V(space::GridFactorSpace, G; closure = PeriodicVelocityClosure())
LowRankFields1D1V(dist::LowRankDistribution, G; closure = PeriodicVelocityClosure())

The workspace of the K-, L- and S-fields of 1D1V Vlasov–Poisson on an nx × nv grid, in the element type T: the bracket arakawa, the $n_x \times n_x$ Green's matrix G of the x-grid, as _green_matrix builds it, the closure closure of the velocity grid, and the kinetic energy kinetic of the velocity nodes v, in T. With the PeriodicVelocityClosure, kinetic = collect(v) .^ 2 ./ 2, and the fields are those of the full-order right-hand side _grid_1d1v_rhs! on the same bracket, G and nodes. With the BoundedVelocityClosure, kinetic is zero at the end nodes $j = 1$ and $j = n_v$, and the fields are those of _grid_1d1v_bounded_rhs!.

The first method throws a DimensionMismatch when size(G) != (nx, nx) or length(v) != nv, and a TypeError for a closure other than PeriodicVelocityClosure and BoundedVelocityClosure. The second builds the bracket Arakawa(nx, nv, step(space.x), step(space.v)) of the grid of space, with (nx, nv) = size(space), and takes the nodes space.v. The third is the second on the space of dist, so it throws a MethodError for a distribution on a space other than a GridFactorSpace, because the stencil and its weights belong to the grid.

The functions _kfield!, _lfield!, _sfield! and _potential! take the workspace.

VlasovMethods._kfield! — Method
_kfield!(K̇, K, V, w::LowRankFields1D1V)
_kfield!(K̇, K, V, w::LowRankFields1D1V, ϕ)

Write the K-field of 1D1V Vlasov–Poisson into K̇ and return it:

\[\dot{K} = h_v \, F(K V^\mathsf{T}) \, V , \qquad F(f) = [h, f] , \qquad h = \phi \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \tfrac{v^2}{2} ,\]

where $F$ is the right-hand side of _grid_1d1v_rhs! on the bracket, G and the velocity nodes of the workspace w, K is nx × r, V is nv × r, and K̇ is nx × r. Without ϕ the potential is that of $f = K V^\mathsf{T}$, from _potential!; with ϕ, a Vector of length nx, the potential is the given ϕ, frozen. The factors are Matrixes of the element type T of w; V need not be orthonormal.

On a workspace with the BoundedVelocityClosure, $F$ is the bounded field $F_b(f) = \chi \circ [P h, \chi \circ f]$ of _grid_1d1v_bounded_rhs!, with $P h = \chi \circ (\phi \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes v^2/2)$ also at a frozen ϕ: rows $1$ and $n_v$ of V are read as zero, and the velocity sums run over the unknowns.

The field is evaluated on the factors, without the $n_x \times n_v$ array: $h$ has rank 2, so each term of Arakawa's stencil projects onto V through $r \times r$ matrices of shifted columns, at a cost of $O((n_x + n_v) r^2)$ beyond the product with G.

Throws a DimensionMismatch before any write when the sizes do not fit the grid of w and the rank r = size(K, 2), and an ArgumentError when K̇ shares memory with K, V or ϕ. At r = 0 it returns the empty K̇. A non-finite entry propagates to the result, unchecked.

_lfield!(L̇, X, L, w::LowRankFields1D1V)

VlasovMethods._potential! — Method
_potential!(ϕ, K, V, w::LowRankFields1D1V)
_potential!(ϕ, X, S, V, w::LowRankFields1D1V)

Write the potential $\phi = G \rho$ of factored data into the Vector ϕ of length nx and return it, with G of the workspace w and the density

\[\rho = h_v K (V^\mathsf{T} 1) \quad \text{of } f = K V^\mathsf{T} , \qquad \rho = h_v X S (V^\mathsf{T} 1) \quad \text{of } f = X S V^\mathsf{T} ,\]

the density $\rho_i = h_v \sum_j f_{ij}$ of _grid_1d1v_rhs!, without the $n_x \times n_v$ array. On a workspace with the BoundedVelocityClosure, $1$ is $\chi$, the indicator of the unknowns $j = 2, …, n_v - 1$, which gives the density $\rho_i = h_v \sum_{j=2}^{n_v-1} f_{ij}$ of _grid_1d1v_bounded_rhs!. The potential of $f = X L^\mathsf{T}$ is _potential!(ϕ, X, L, w). The factors are Matrixes of the element type T of w.

Throws a DimensionMismatch before any write when the sizes do not fit the grid of w and the rank, and an ArgumentError when ϕ shares memory with a factor. At the rank 0 it writes zeros. A non-finite entry propagates to the result, unchecked.

VlasovMethods._sfield! — Method
_sfield!(Ṡ, X, S, V, w::LowRankFields1D1V)
_sfield!(Ṡ, X, S, V, w::LowRankFields1D1V, ϕ)

Write the S-field of 1D1V Vlasov–Poisson into Ṡ and return it:

\[\dot{S} = h_x h_v \, X^\mathsf{T} F(X S V^\mathsf{T}) \, V ,\]

The step and the run driver

VlasovMethods.LowRankIntegrator — Type
LowRankIntegrator(Y, method, field, tspan, tstep)

The run of a dynamical low-rank method from the LowRankDistribution Y over the span tspan = (t₀, t₁) with the step tstep, for a method with a lowrank_step! on the workspace field that keeps the rank, or for AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax), whose rank stays within rmax; run! refuses a step that grows the rank beyond. source
VlasovMethods.lowrank_step! — Function
lowrank_step!(Y, method, field, t, τ)

Advance the LowRankDistribution Y by one step of length τ from the time t of the dynamical low-rank method, in place, and return Y. field is the right-hand side of the method. The methods are BUG, AugmentedBUG and MidpointBUG, and ProjectorSplitting; the docstring of each method names its algorithm and the fields that it takes.

t and τ are converted to the element type T of Y before their first use. A τ that is not finite in T throws an ArgumentError before any write, whose message prints τ as it was given: on a Float32 state, the finite τ = 1e300 reads got τ = 1.0e300. The step assigns new factors to Y, possibly of another rank, and sets nfixed = 0. The entries of Y are not checked.

LowRankIntegrator and run! take a run of steps.

VlasovMethods.run! — Method
run!(int::LowRankIntegrator, h5file)

Advance the state Y of int over the times t₀:tstep:t₁, the initial time included, in place with lowrank_step!(Y, method, field, tₙ, tstep), write every state into the HDF5 file h5file, and return Y. The file is created, or overwritten, and closed on return, also when a step throws. Before the file is opened, run! throws an ArgumentError for a state whose rank is outside rmin:rmax of an AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax), for a tstep that is zero or not finite in the element type of Y, and for an element type of the times or of Y that HDF5 does not store, such as Int128, Float16 or BigFloat; an existing file is then left unchanged.

Time t₀ is the initial state, written before any step, and step s is the s-th call of lowrank_step!, which writes the time t₀ + s · tstep. The file holds the datasets t of the nt times, rank of the nt ranks, and X of size (nx, R, nt), S of size (R, R, nt) and V of size (nv, R, nt), zero beyond the rank of each state, with R the rank of Y or, for AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax), min(rmax, nx, nv).

A step that leaves all(isfinite, Y.S) false stops the run without an error: nothing is written for it, its rank and factors stay zero, and the file attribute status is "failed at step <s>". A step that leaves a rank r > R stops the run the same way, with status set to "failed at step <s>: rank <r> beyond the <R> stored columns", and then throws an ArgumentError. A step that throws sets status to "failed at step <s>: <E>", with E the name of the type of the exception, and the exception propagates from run!. A run that completes sets status to "ok".

The BUG integrators

VlasovMethods.AugmentedBUG — Type
AugmentedBUG()
AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin = 1, rmax)

The augmented basis-update & Galerkin integrator of Ceruti, Kusch & Lubich 2022 (doi 10.1007/s10543-021-00907-7), a reference method of dynamical low rank. One step of length τ from X₀ S₀ V₀ᵀ takes the K- and L-steps of BUG, then

  1. X̂ and V̂, the weighted orthonormal bases of [K(τ) X₀] and [L(τ) V₀], of min(2r, nx) and min(2r, nv) columns;
  2. the S-step on X̂ and V̂ over τ from (h_x X̂ᵀX₀) S₀ (h_v V₀ᵀV̂);
  3. the SVD Ŝ(τ) = P Σ Qᵀ and the truncation X₁ = X̂ P₁, S₁ = Diagonal(σ₁, …, σ_{r₁}), V₁ = V̂ Q₁ to the first r₁ singular vectors, so S₁ is diagonal and non-increasing.

AugmentedBUG() truncates to the rank r of the step's input. AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax) truncates to r₁ = clamp(r*, rmin, min(rmax, k)), with k the number of singular values of Ŝ(τ) and r* the least r with $(\sum_{j > r} \sigma_j^2)^{1/2} \le$ tol: tol is an absolute tolerance in the weighted Frobenius norm, which is the Frobenius norm of S for orthonormal bases. A step of this method throws an ArgumentError before any write when the rank of its input lies outside rmin:rmax.

Throws an ArgumentError unless tol is a finite real ≥ 0 and rmin and rmax are integers with 1 ≤ rmin ≤ rmax. lowrank_step! takes the step.

source
VlasovMethods.BUG — Type
BUG()

The fixed-rank basis-update & Galerkin integrator of Ceruti & Lubich 2022 (doi 10.1007/s10543-021-00873-0), a reference method of dynamical low rank. One step of length τ from X₀ S₀ V₀ᵀ, with $F$ the right-hand side of _grid_1d1v_rhs! and the weights $h_x$, $h_v$ of the grid:

  1. the K-step $\dot{K} = h_v F(K V_0^\mathsf{T}) V_0$ from X₀ S₀ and the L-step $\dot{L} = h_x F(X_0 L^\mathsf{T})^\mathsf{T} X_0$ from V₀ S₀ᵀ, both over τ;
  2. X₁ and V₁, the weighted orthonormal bases of K(τ) and L(τ);
  3. the S-step $\dot{S} = h_x h_v X_1^\mathsf{T} F(X_1 S V_1^\mathsf{T}) V_1$ over τ from M S₀ Nᵀ, with M = h_x X₁ᵀX₀ and N = h_v V₁ᵀV₀.

The rank stays that of the input. lowrank_step! takes the step.

source
VlasovMethods.MidpointBUG — Type
MidpointBUG()

The midpoint basis-update & Galerkin integrator, "Midpoint BUG (4r)" of Ceruti, Einkemmer, Kusch & Lubich 2024 (doi 10.1007/s10543-024-01032-x; arXiv 2402.08607v1, §3), a reference method of dynamical low rank, truncated to the rank r of its input. One step of length τ from Y₀ = X₀ S₀ V₀ᵀ:

  1. the step of AugmentedBUG over τ/2, without its truncation, gives Û, Ŝ½, V̂ and Ŷ½ = Û Ŝ½ V̂ᵀ;
  2. Ū and V̄, the weighted orthonormal bases of [Û, τ h_v F(Ŷ½) V̂] and [V̂, τ h_x F(Ŷ½)ᵀ Û], of up to 4r columns, and the Galerkin step $\dot{S} = h_x h_v \bar{U}^\mathsf{T} F(\bar{U} S \bar{V}^\mathsf{T}) \bar{V}$ over τ from (h_x ŪᵀX₀) S₀ (h_v V₀ᵀV̄);
  3. the SVD of S̄(τ) and the truncation to r, as in AugmentedBUG.

lowrank_step! takes the step.

VlasovMethods.lowrank_step! — Method
lowrank_step!(Y, method::Union{BUG, AugmentedBUG, MidpointBUG}, field, t, τ)

The step of BUG, AugmentedBUG or MidpointBUG on the LowRankDistribution Y on a GridFactorSpace. field is the workspace VlasovMethods.LowRankFields1D1V of the same element type T, whose K-, L- and S-fields evaluate the full-order field on the factors, without the nx × nv array. The fields are autonomous, so t is not read. Each K-, L-, S- and Galerkin substep is one step of the classical RK4 tableau over its interval. The contract of every step, the conversion of t and τ and the refusal of a non-finite τ, is that of the generic lowrank_step!.

τ = 0 keeps X S Vᵀ to round-off, except for AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax), whose truncation still applies and changes X S Vᵀ by up to tol in the weighted Frobenius norm. The step counts every column of V in the rank, the fixed modes of Y included, and sets nfixed = 0, because no reference method keeps the fixed modes in the span of V.

An S-step or a Galerkin step that leaves a non-finite value does not throw. BUG() keeps its factors of rank r, non-finite as they come; the augmented and the midpoint BUG skip the truncation and install the first r columns of the augmented bases and an r × r core of NaN, with r the rank of the input. The status that a caller tests is all(isfinite, Y.S).

The projector splitting

VlasovMethods.ProjectorSplitting — Type
ProjectorSplitting(composition)

The projector-splitting integrator of dynamical low rank (Lubich and Oseledets 2014, doi:10.1007/s10543-013-0454-0), a method for lowrank_step!. composition is :lie, the first-order K–S–L splitting, or :strang, the second-order splitting with the prediction of the midpoint potential of Einkemmer and Lubich 2018 (doi:10.1137/18m116383x, Algorithm 1). The two compositions are instances of two different types. Each substep is one step of the classical RK4 tableau over the substep's interval.

Throws an ArgumentError for any other composition, a String included.

source
VlasovMethods.lowrank_step! — Method
lowrank_step!(Y::LowRankDistribution, method::ProjectorSplitting, field, t, τ)

Advance the low-rank state Y by one step of length τ from the time t, in place, and return Y. field is the right-hand side, such as the LowRankFields1D1V of the state's grid. With $F$ the full-order right-hand side, the substeps are those of Lubich and Oseledets 2014:

\[\dot{K} = h_v F(K V^\mathsf{T}) V , \qquad \dot{S} = -h_x h_v X^\mathsf{T} F(X S V^\mathsf{T}) V , \qquad \dot{L} = h_x F(X L^\mathsf{T})^\mathsf{T} X ,\]

each one RK4 step. After a K-substep, X, S = orthonormalize_x(Y.space, K); after an L-substep, V, R = orthonormalize_v(Y.space, L) and S = Rᵀ, so X and V are orthonormal in the weighted products of the space, $h_x X^\mathsf{T} X = I$ and $h_v V^\mathsf{T} V = I$.

  • :lie runs the K-substep over $[t, t + τ]$ from $K = X S$, the backward S-substep over $[t, t + τ]$, and the L-substep over $[t, t + τ]$ from $L = V S^\mathsf{T}$.
  • :strang runs Algorithm 1 of Einkemmer and Lubich 2018: the K- and the backward S-substep over $[t, t + τ/2]$; the potential $\phi^0$ of $X S V^\mathsf{T}$ with the X and S of these two substeps and the V of the start; an L-substep over $[t, t + τ/2]$ at the frozen $\phi^0$, which predicts $L^{1/2}$; the potential $\phi^{1/2}$ of $X (L^{1/2})^\mathsf{T}$; the L-substep over $[t, t + τ]$ at the frozen $\phi^{1/2}$; and the backward S- and the K-substep over $[t + τ/2, t + τ]$. The K- and S-substeps take the self-consistent field.

The step needs no inverse of S, so a rank-deficient S, the zero state included, is taken. It forms no $n_x \times n_v$ array, writes only Y, assigns new factors to it, and sets Y.nfixed = 0: the splitting does not keep fixed velocity modes in the leading columns of V.

τ is a signed step; τ = 0 leaves X S Vᵀ unchanged up to round-off, while the factors can change. A non-finite entry of Y propagates. The contract of every step, the conversion of t and τ and the refusal of a non-finite τ, is that of the generic lowrank_step!.

A field of another kind joins the step through the methods _lowrank_kfield!(K̇, K, V, field, t), _lowrank_sfield!(Ṡ, X, S, V, field, t) (with the plus sign, $h_x h_v X^\mathsf{T} F V$) and _lowrank_lfield!(L̇, X, L, field, t), and for :strang also _lowrank_potential(X, S, V, field, t), _lowrank_potential(X, L, field, t) and _lowrank_lfield!(L̇, X, L, field, t, ϕ).
