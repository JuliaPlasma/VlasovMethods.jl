Reduced-order models

A reduced-order model replaces the $2 N$ particle coordinates of a Vlasov–Poisson run by a few coefficients in a reduced basis. The basis is learned from snapshots of full-order runs at several values of a parameter, and the reduced model then runs at new values of that parameter. This page describes the building blocks; Symplectic model reduction runs the experiments of the paper with them.

The workflow has four stages.

  1. Sweep. March the full-order model at every sample of a parameter space, and save the states into Snapshots.
  2. Bases. Compute a symplectic basis of the particle snapshots with particle_basis, and a POD basis of the field snapshots with field_basis.
  3. Reduced march. March the reduced model with a reduced electric field, either the full field of the lifted particles, ReducedElectricField, or its hyper-reduction by the discrete empirical interpolation method, DEIMElectricField.
  4. Diagnostics. Compare the full-order and the reduced runs with the functions of Diagnostics.

The bases and the DEIM indices come from ReducedBasisMethods, and the parameter spaces from ReducedComplexityModeling.

The electric field

The marches on this page take the field as an ElectricField. Its interface is two functions: update!(f, x, w, t) deposits the positions x with the weights w and solves for the potential, and efield!(f, e, x) writes the field at the positions x into e. A field holds its own buffers, so neither call allocates.

fieldevaluates
PoissonField(potential)the full-order field of a Potential of PoissonSolvers
ScaledField(field, χ)the field divided by $\chi^2$, for the rescaled time of a parameter sweep
ReducedElectricField(field, Ψ, X)lift by $\Psi$, solve, evaluate at every particle, project by $\Psi^{\mathsf T}$
DEIMElectricField(field, Ψ, Ψₑ, p, X)the same, evaluated at the $k_e$ DEIM points p of the field basis $\Psi_e$ only

energy returns the field energy $\frac{1}{2} \int (\partial_x \phi)^2 \, dx$ of a field after its last update!.

The parameter scale

The bump-on-tail sweep varies the wave number $\kappa$. The particles are drawn once, at the reference wave number $\kappa_0$, and a sample at $\kappa$ marches them on the same domain with the scale $\chi = \kappa / \kappa_0$. The scale enters the march as a time-step scale, $\Delta t \, \chi$, and the field of the march as $E / \chi^2$. Every march reads $\chi$ from its parameters, params.χ, and a ScaledField must be built with the same value; a march throws an ArgumentError where the two differ.

Sweeps and snapshots

Snapshots holds the positions X, the velocities V, the accelerations A and the potentials Φ of every saved state of every sample, and the field energy W, the kinetic energy K and the momentum M. IntegratorParameters holds the time step and the counts of the sweep. integrate_vp marches the full-order model of one sample with the leapfrog method, and saves its states:

using PoissonSolvers
using ReducedComplexityModeling: Parameter, ParameterSpace
using VlasovMethods

κ₀ = 0.3
pspace = ParameterSpace(Parameter(:χ, collect(range(0.1, 0.5; length = 10) ./ κ₀)))

particles = initialize!(ParticleDistribution(1, 1, 10_000), BumpOnTail(κ = κ₀)).particles
poisson = Potential(PeriodicBasisSpline((0.0, 2π / κ₀), 4, 16))   # cubic: the order is 4

dt, nt = 0.1, 250
nh = length(PoissonSolvers.coefficients(poisson))
IP = IntegratorParameters(dt, nt, nt + 1, nh, length(particles), length(pspace))
SS = Snapshots(Float64, 1, length(particles), nh, nt + 1, pspace)

for p in 1:length(pspace)
    params = pspace(p)
    field = ScaledField(PoissonField(poisson), params.χ)
    integrate_vp(particles, params, field, SS, IP, p)
end

The march does not write the accelerations SS.A, which the field basis reduces. The field of each sample, deposited from the saved positions and evaluated at them, gives them, as in the script scripts/bump_on_tail_training.jl:

using VlasovMethods: efield!, update!

w = vec(particles.w)
a = zeros(size(SS.A, 2))
for p in 1:length(pspace), ts in axes(SS.A, 3)
    field = ScaledField(PoissonField(poisson), pspace(p).χ)
    x = @view SS.X[1, :, ts, p]
    update!(field, x, w, 0.0)
    SS.A[1, :, ts, p] .= efield!(field, a, x)
end

h5save(io, SS) and Snapshots(io) write and read the snapshots in HDF5.

Reduced bases

particle_basis stacks the positions over the velocities, one column per saved state and sample, and passes the snapshot matrix to the ReducedBasis of ReducedBasisMethods with the given algorithm. A cotangent lift, CotangentLiftSVD() or CotangentLiftEVD(), gives a symplectic basis. field_basis computes the POD basis of the accelerations.

using ReducedBasisMethods: CotangentLiftEVD, PODEVD, get_DEIM_indices

Φ = particle_basis(CotangentLiftEVD(), SS; tolerance = 1e-9).Φ
Ψₑ = field_basis(PODEVD(), SS; tolerance = 1e-5).Φ
p = get_DEIM_indices(Ψₑ)

The reduced march

reduced_integrate_vp marches the reduced coordinates $\Phi^{\mathsf T} x$ and $\Phi^{\mathsf T} v$ with the same leapfrog method as the full-order march, and saves the lifted states into its own snapshots. It takes a ReducedIntegratorCache of the basis size, which one march after another can share:

IC = ReducedIntegratorCache(IP, size(Φ, 2))
X₀ = Φ' * vec(particles.x)
ROM = Snapshots(Float64, 1, length(particles), nh, nt + 1, pspace)

for s in 1:length(pspace)
    params = pspace(s)
    field = DEIMElectricField(ScaledField(PoissonField(poisson), params.χ), Φ, Ψₑ, p, X₀)
    reduced_integrate_vp(particles, Φ, params, field, ROM, IP, IC, s)
end

Both marches are inferred and allocate nothing inside the time loop.

Diagnostics

The diagnostics take arrays rather than snapshots, so they serve any data of the same shape.

functionreturns
trajectory_errorsthe root-mean-square and the maximum errors of the positions and the velocities
potential_errorthe error of the potential coefficients
field_energy_errorthe relative error of the field energy
energy_driftthe relative drift of an energy history
growth_ratethe growth rate of a field energy history, fitted to its maxima
best_and_worstthe samples whose growth rates agree best and worst
saved_indicesthe final, three-quarter and half-way saves of a sweep

The analytic quadratic model

The quadratic potential $H(q, p) = \frac{1}{2} |p|^2 + \frac{1}{2} \beta^2 |q|^2$ has a closed solution, which tests a reduction without the error of a field solve. AnalyticHamiltonian(β) is its Hamiltonian and its gradient, in the form that the reduced ODE and PODE problems of ReducedBasisMethods take. lift_solution lifts a reduced PODE solution back to the positions and the velocities. scripts/analytic_quadratic.jl runs the experiment.

The Hamiltonian neural network

train_hnn trains a Hamiltonian neural network on the reduced field of a sweep: its gradient is fitted to the reduced accelerations. It is a package extension, which loads with GeometricOptimizers and AbstractNeuralNetworks. scripts/bump_on_tail_train_hnn.jl trains it on the coefficients of the bump-on-tail sweep.
