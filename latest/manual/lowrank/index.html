Dynamical low rank

A dynamical low-rank method evolves a 1D1V distribution function in the factored form

\[f(x, v) \approx \sum_{a, b = 1}^{r} X_a(x) \, S_{ab} \, V_b(v) ,\]

with $r$ x factors $X_a$, $r$ v factors $V_b$ and an $r \times r$ coefficient matrix $S$. The state costs $(n_x + n_v) r + r^2$ numbers in place of $n_x n_v$.

The factor space

The factors live in a factor space, a subtype of VlasovMethods.AbstractFactorSpace. The space gives the lengths of the factors, nx, nv = size(space), and the weighted inner products of the x and the v factors with their orthonormalisations: VlasovMethods.inner_x, VlasovMethods.inner_v, VlasovMethods.orthonormalize_x and VlasovMethods.orthonormalize_v. These four functions and the abstract type are documented, but not exported.

GridFactorSpace is the factor space of the nodes of a GridDistribution: a factor is a vector of its values at the nodes, and the inner products are the rectangle rules of the grid, $\langle a, b \rangle_x = h_x \sum_i a_i b_i$ and $\langle a, b \rangle_v = h_v \sum_j a_j b_j$; the rule in $v$ is the rule of velocity_moments of a grid distribution. The orthonormalisations return Q and R of A = Q R, with $h_x Q^\mathsf{T} Q = I$ or $h_v Q^\mathsf{T} Q = I$.

The low-rank distribution

LowRankDistribution holds the factor space, the factors X, S and V, and the number nfixed of leading columns of V that span fixed velocity modes. It factorises the node values of a grid distribution at a rank r. With fixed = [1 v v²], the first three columns of V span 1, $v$ and $v^2$, and the rest of V comes from the weighted truncated SVD of the part of $f$ outside them. The factorisation is then the weighted orthogonal projection of $f$ onto the span of V, which keeps the density, the momentum and the energy of $f$:

using VlasovMethods

nx, nv = 32, 64
dist = GridDistribution(zeros(nx, nv), (0.0, 4π), (-10.0, 10.0))
dist.values .= [exp(-(v - 0.5sin(x / 2))^2 / 2) / sqrt(2π) for x in dist.x, v in dist.v]

v = collect(dist.v)
d = LowRankDistribution(dist, 5; fixed = hcat(one.(v), v, v .^ 2))
m, md = velocity_moments(dist), velocity_moments(d)
(rank = size(d.S, 1), nfixed = d.nfixed,
 moment_errors = map((a, b) -> maximum(abs, a - b) / maximum(abs, a), m, md))
(rank = 5, nfixed = 3, moment_errors = (density = 1.1102230246251561e-15, momentum = 1.2039686473299093e-15, energy = 3.996802888650563e-14))

Without fixed modes the factorisation is the weighted truncated SVD of rank r, and the moments are not kept. Where the part of $f$ outside the fixed modes has a numerical rank below r - nfixed, the remaining columns of V come from a Householder QR, with no random numbers, so a factorisation is the same for every state of the global random number generator.

velocity_moments(d) and evaluate(d, x, v) work on the factors and never form the $n_x \times n_v$ array; GridDistribution(d) forms it, for tests and diagnostics:

f = GridDistribution(d)
maximum(abs, f.values - dist.values) / maximum(abs, dist.values)
0.028970500477934515

A step of a method replaces the factors of the mutable LowRankDistribution, also by factors of another rank. The factors are Matrix{DT} values, with DT the element type of the space. LowRankDistribution(space, X, S, V; nfixed) takes factors on any factor space, stores a Matrix{DT} factor without a copy and converts any other AbstractMatrix{DT}, such as a view or a Diagonal, to a new Matrix{DT}. It checks their sizes but not their orthonormality.

The factored fields

A dynamical low-rank step evolves the factors through the projections of the full-order right-hand side $F(f) = [h, f]$ of the grid model, with $h = \phi \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes v^2/2$: the K-field $\dot{K} = h_v F(K V^\mathsf{T}) V$, the L-field $\dot{L} = h_x F(X L^\mathsf{T})^\mathsf{T} X$ and the S-field $\dot{S} = h_x h_v X^\mathsf{T} F(X S V^\mathsf{T}) V$. The workspace VlasovMethods.LowRankFields1D1V holds the Arakawa bracket, the Green's matrix and the kinetic energy $v^2/2$ of the grid, and the functions VlasovMethods._kfield!, VlasovMethods._lfield!, VlasovMethods._sfield! and VlasovMethods._potential! evaluate the K-, L- and S-fields and the potential on the factors, without forming $f$. The Hamiltonian $h$ has rank 2, so each term of Arakawa's stencil projects onto the other factor through $r \times r$ matrices of shifted columns, and a field costs $O((n_x + n_v) r^2)$ beyond the product with the Green's matrix. Each field also takes a given, frozen potential $\phi$. The workspace and the functions are documented, but not exported.

The closure of the fields

The workspace takes the closure of the velocity grid as the keyword closure of each of its constructors, as the grid model does. The default PeriodicVelocityClosure() gives the fields above. With BoundedVelocityClosure(), the fields are those of the bounded field $F_b(f) = \chi \circ [P h, \chi \circ f]$ of the grid model, from the section Bounded velocity closure. The cut Hamiltonian $P h = \phi \otimes \chi + 1 \otimes (\chi \circ v^2/2)$ has rank 2 too, so the fields keep their factored form: the velocity sums run over the unknowns $j = 2, \dots, n_v - 1$, the term of $\phi$ is weighted by $\chi$ at the velocity neighbour, the kinetic energy is cut to the unknowns, and the density is $\rho = h_v K (V^\mathsf{T} \chi)$. Rows $1$ and $n_v$ of V and L are read as zero, and rows $1$ and $n_v$ of $\dot{L}$ are zero. No field forms $f$. With X and V orthonormal and the end rows of V zero, the DLR field keeps the energy when $\chi$ and $\chi \circ v^2$ lie in the span of V.

The BUG integrators

The basis-update & Galerkin (BUG) integrators are the reference methods of dynamical low rank. Each step evolves the factors through the K-, L- and S-fields, and none forms $f$:

  • BUG is the fixed-rank integrator of Ceruti & Lubich 2022 (doi:10.1007/s10543-021-00873-0): the K- and L-steps from the old bases give the new bases, and the S-step runs on them.
  • AugmentedBUG is the rank-adaptive integrator of Ceruti, Kusch & Lubich 2022 (doi:10.1007/s10543-021-00907-7): the S-step runs on the new bases augmented by the old ones, and the SVD of its result truncates the step, to the rank of the input or by a tolerance on the discarded singular values.
  • MidpointBUG is the second-order "Midpoint BUG (4r)" of Ceruti, Einkemmer, Kusch & Lubich 2024 (doi:10.1007/s10543-024-01032-x), truncated to the rank of its input.

lowrank_step!(Y, method, field, t, τ) takes one step in place on a LowRankDistribution, with the workspace of the fields; each substep is one step of the classical RK4 tableau. A step counts the fixed modes in the rank and sets nfixed = 0, because no BUG integrator keeps the fixed modes in the span of V. A step whose S-step or Galerkin step leaves a non-finite value does not throw; all(isfinite, Y.S) is its status. At the full rank $r = n_x$ with $n_x \le n_v \le 2 n_x$, every projection of AugmentedBUG() and MidpointBUG() is the identity, and a step is the RK4 step of the full-order model; AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax) truncates at full rank too, so its step differs from it by up to tol in the weighted Frobenius norm:

using PoissonSolvers: FFTWBasis, Potential

model = VlasovPoisson(dist, Potential(FFTWBasis((0.0, 4π), nx)))
Y = LowRankDistribution(dist, nx)
field = VlasovMethods.LowRankFields1D1V(Y, model.work)
lowrank_step!(Y, AugmentedBUG(), field, 0.0, 0.05)
(rank = size(Y.S, 1), nfixed = Y.nfixed, status = all(isfinite, Y.S))
(rank = 32, nfixed = 0, status = true)

LowRankIntegrator(Y, method, field, tspan, tstep) holds a run of a method whose lowrank_step! keeps the rank, or of AugmentedBUG(tol; rmin, rmax), whose rank stays within rmax. run! writes the times, the ranks and the factors of every state into an HDF5 file, with the file attribute status, and it stops with an error, after it sets status, at a step that grows the rank beyond the columns that it stores.

The projector splitting

ProjectorSplitting is the projector-splitting integrator of Lubich and Oseledets, a reference method: it splits the tangent projector of the low-rank manifold into a K-substep with V fixed, a backward S-substep, and an L-substep with X fixed, and orthonormalises K and L by a QR in the weighted products of the factor space. ProjectorSplitting(:lie) is the first-order composition; ProjectorSplitting(:strang) is the second-order composition of Einkemmer and Lubich, which runs its L-substeps at a frozen potential and predicts the midpoint potential by a half step. Each substep is one RK4 step. lowrank_step! advances a LowRankDistribution by one step in place, with the fields of the grid as the right-hand side, and sets nfixed = 0, because the splitting does not keep fixed velocity modes:

w = VlasovMethods.LowRankFields1D1V(d.space, model.work)
Y = LowRankDistribution(dist, 5)
method = ProjectorSplitting(:strang)
for n in 0:9
    lowrank_step!(Y, method, w, 0.05n, 0.05)
end
(rank = size(Y.S, 1), nfixed = Y.nfixed,
 orthonormality = maximum(abs, VlasovMethods.inner_x(Y.space, Y.X, Y.X) - one(Y.S)))
(rank = 5, nfixed = 0, orthonormality = 4.440892098500626e-16)

The step takes any field with methods of the internal functions _lowrank_kfield!, _lowrank_sfield! and _lowrank_lfield!, and for :strang also _lowrank_potential, which the docstring of lowrank_step! lists.
