Landau collisions

The Landau operator describes the collisions of charged particles. For one species in two velocity dimensions it reads

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \nu \, \nabla_v \cdot \int U(v - v') \, \bigl( f(v') \nabla_v f(v) - f(v) \nabla_{v'} f(v') \bigr) \, dv' ,\]

with the kernel

\[U(u) = |u|^{\gamma + 2} \left( \mathbb{I} - \frac{u \otimes u}{|u|^2} \right) .\]

The exponent $\gamma = -3$ is the Coulomb case, and $\gamma = 0$ gives the Maxwell molecules. kernel evaluates $U$. The operator conserves the mass, the momentum and the energy, and it decreases the entropy $\int f \log f \, dv$.

The MP-L manuscript writes the operator as a metric bracket and discretises it on particles. The package implements both of its methods. Both project the particles onto a spline $f_s$ of two velocity dimensions, and both use the gradient of the discrete entropy $S_h = \int f_s \log |f_s| \, dv$.

The particle-pair method

PairwiseLandau is the gradient system

\[\dot{v}_\gamma = - \nu \sum_{\alpha \ne \gamma} w_\alpha \, U(v_\gamma - v_\alpha) \bigl( \psi_\gamma - \psi_\alpha \bigr) , \qquad \psi_\alpha = \sum_k \mathbb{L}_k \nabla \varphi_k(v_\alpha) ,\]

where $\mathbb{L}_k$ is the $L^2$ projection of $1 + \log |f_s|$ onto the spline basis (compute_J!). The sum over the particle pairs makes the matrix $\mathbb{G}$ of apply_G! symmetric and negative semi-definite, and its null space holds the momentum and the energy. The cost of one evaluation is $O(N^2)$ kernel evaluations.

discrete_gradient_step! takes one step of the discrete gradient method

\[\frac{v^{n+1} - v^n}{\Delta t} = \nu \, \mathbb{G}\!\left( \frac{v^n + v^{n+1}}{2} \right) \bar{\nabla} S_h(v^n, v^{n+1}) ,\]

solved by a fixed-point iteration to the absolute residual abstol. The step writes the new velocities into the particles, and returns the number of iterations and the final residual. It conserves the momentum to rounding and the energy to the tolerance of the iteration. The discrete gradient gives the discrete H-theorem at the fixed point of the iteration, which the accepted state misses by a residual of at most abstol. Two discrete gradients are implemented: the GonzalezGradient, the default and the choice of the manuscript, gives it to rounding, and the AVFGradient, the average of the gradient along the segment, to the quadrature error of its rule.

The example below relaxes the BKW profile of 200 particles over 20 steps, on quadratic splines of 8 cells per axis:

using VlasovMethods

dist = initialize!(ParticleDistribution(1, 2, 200), BKWDistribution(0.2))

# the velocity domain covers the sample with a margin
halfwidth = 1.5 * maximum(abs, dist.particles.v)
sdist = SplineDistribution(1, 2, 9, 3, (-halfwidth, halfwidth))

model = PairwiseLandau(dist, CollisionEntropy(sdist); γ = -3, ν = 1.0)

w = vec(dist.particles.w)
entropy = CollisionEntropy(sdist)
function diagnostics(v)
    projection(v, dist, sdist)
    return (momentum = v * w, energy = sum(w .* vec(sum(abs2, v; dims = 1))) / 2,
        entropy = entropy())
end

before = diagnostics(copy(dist.particles.v))
iterations = [discrete_gradient_step!(model, 1e-4; abstol = 1e-10).iterations for k in 1:20]
after = diagnostics(copy(dist.particles.v))

(iterations = iterations,
 momentum_drift = maximum(abs, after.momentum .- before.momentum),
 energy_drift = abs(after.energy - before.energy) / before.energy,
 entropy = (before.entropy, after.entropy))
(iterations = [4, 4, 5, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 5, 8, 5], momentum_drift = 1.3877787807814457e-17, energy_drift = 4.2216711594549707e-16, entropy = (-2.8112447237328655, -2.8115056301763017))

The momentum and the energy drift at the level of rounding, and the entropy decreases.

Errors

The step refuses what the scheme cannot handle. Two particles of equal velocities throw a DomainError, because the kernel of a particle pair at $u = 0$ is not defined; for $\gamma < -2$ so do two velocities whose distance is too small for the kernel to be finite. A particle that leaves the velocity domain of the spline throws the DomainError of the projection. A fixed-point iteration that does not reach abstol in max_iters iterations throws an ErrorException. In each case, the particles keep the velocities of the last accepted step. The docstring of discrete_gradient_step! lists the residual floor that bounds a useful abstol.

The spline method

Landau is the method of the appendix of MP-L. It assembles the spline matrix

\[\mathbb{L}_{ij} = \frac{1}{2} \int\!\!\int \bigl( \nabla \varphi_i(v) - \nabla \varphi_i(v') \bigr) \cdot |f_s(v)| \, U(v - v') \, |f_s(v')| \, \bigl( \nabla \varphi_j(v) - \nabla \varphi_j(v') \bigr) \, dv \, dv'\]

of compute_L! on the tensor-product quadrature grid, at a cost of $O(Q^2)$ kernel evaluations for $Q$ quadrature points, and the matrices $\mathbb{K}_{k,\alpha} = w_\alpha \nabla \varphi_k(v_\alpha)$ of compute_K!. The velocities follow from the least-squares problem $\mathbb{K} \dot{v} = \mathbb{L} \, \mathbb{L}_k$. The appendix needs $\mathbb{K} \mathbb{K}^{+} = \mathbb{I}$, which requires at least as many particle velocity components as basis functions less one, $dN \ge M - 1$, and a particle in the support of every basis function.

Picard_iterate_Landau_nls! takes one implicit-midpoint step, solved by a Picard iteration. The implicit midpoint rule is the appendix's choice. It is not a discrete gradient, so no discrete H-theorem holds for the spline method: the entropy of a step need not decrease. The discrete H-theorem holds for the particle-pair method with its discrete gradient step. The caller keeps the velocities of the two previous steps and the two previous vector fields:

landau = Landau(dist, CollisionEntropy(sdist); γ = -3, ν = 1.0)

v = copy(dist.particles.v)
v_prev_2 = copy(v)
rhs = zeros(size(v)..., 2)
for k in 1:nsteps
    VlasovMethods.Picard_iterate_Landau_nls!(landau, Δt, k, k * Δt, v, v_prev_2, rhs;
        abstol = 1e-10)
    v_prev_2 .= v
    v .= dist.particles.v
end
Known issue of the spline method

The implementation solves the least-squares problem for each velocity component alone, and its vector field has the opposite sign of the appendix's $-\mathbb{K}^{+} \mathbb{L} \, \mathbb{L}_k$. Under it, the temperature anisotropy grows at the rate at which it should decay, and the energy drifts by $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-7}$ in the runs of Particle methods for the Landau operator. K96 of KNOWN_ISSUES.md records the measurements. The particle-pair method has neither defect.

The reference cases

The three two-dimensional initial conditions of Initial conditions are the reference cases of the operator: the AnisotropicBiMaxwellian, whose temperatures isotropise at the closed rate $8$ for $\gamma = 0$, the DoubleMaxwellian2D, and the BKWDistribution, an exact solution for $\gamma = 0$. The script scripts/reference_forms.jl derives their closed forms, and Particle methods for the Landau operator runs them under both methods.
