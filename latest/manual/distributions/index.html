Distribution functions

The distribution function $f(x, v)$ of a species is the state of every model in the package. VlasovMethods stores it in one of three forms. Each form is a subtype of DistributionFunction{DT, XD, VD}, with the element type DT, the number XD of position dimensions and the number VD of velocity dimensions.

typestoresused by
ParticleDistributionpositions, velocities and weights of $N$ particlesevery particle model
SplineDistributionthe coefficients of a spline in $v$the collision operators, the entropy
GridDistributionthe values at the nodes of a 1D1V phase-space gridthe grid Vlasov–Poisson model, the reduced tensors

The collision operators use two of them at once: the particles carry the state, and a spline holds the projection of the particles, from which the operator computes $f_s'/f_s$ or $\log |f_s|$. Projections describes the conversions between the forms.

A spline or a grid distribution is a callable: dist(z...) evaluates it at the phase-space point z, the XD position components first and the VD velocity components after them.

Particles

A ParticleDistribution is the Klimontovich measure

\[f_p(x, v) = \sum_{\alpha = 1}^{N} w_\alpha \, \delta(x - x_\alpha) \, \delta(v - v_\alpha)\]

of $N$ particles. ParticleDistribution(xdim, vdim, npart) allocates the particles of xdim position and vdim velocity components, all zero:

using VlasovMethods

dist = ParticleDistribution(1, 1, 5)
dist.particles.x .= [0.1 0.3 0.5 0.7 0.9]
dist.particles.v .= [-1.0 -0.5 0.0 0.5 1.0]
dist.particles.w .= 1 / 5
dist.particles.z
2×5 view(::Matrix{Float64}, 1:2, :) with eltype Float64:
  0.1   0.3  0.5  0.7  0.9
 -1.0  -0.5  0.0  0.5  1.0

The particles are a ParticleList of ParticleMethods. It stores one matrix with one column per particle, and gives four views of it:

  • particles.x, the xdim × N positions,
  • particles.v, the vdim × N velocities,
  • particles.z, the (xdim + vdim) × N phase-space coordinates, positions first,
  • particles.w, the 1 × N weights.

A write to one view changes the others. The splitting methods advance particles.z, and the collision operators advance particles.v.

Splines in velocity

A SplineDistribution holds a B-spline in the velocity,

\[f_s(v) = \sum_I \hat{f}_I \, \varphi_I(v) ,\]

with one basis function per index $I$ in one velocity dimension and a tensor-product basis in more. It has no position dependence. The constructor takes the dimensions, the number of breakpoints, the spline order, the velocity domain, the width of an optional oversized end cell, and the boundary condition:

using VlasovMethods

sdist = SplineDistribution(1, 1, 41, 4, (-10.0, 10.0))
SplineDistribution{Float64, xdim=1, vdim=1}((43,))

The order is $k = p + 1$, the convention of SimpleSplines: order 4 is the cubic basis that the collision manuscripts use. A clamped basis of order $k$ on $n$ breakpoints has $n + k - 2$ basis functions, so the basis above has 43 of them:

nbasis(sdist.basis)
43

The fields of a spline distribution are the spline, the basis, the quadrature and the coefficients. The spline shares its coefficient array, so a projection that writes the coefficients changes the function that sdist.spline evaluates, without a rebuild:

project_Maxwellian(sdist)
(sdist.spline(0.0), 1 / sqrt(2π))
(0.399081847680752, 0.3989422804014327)

In two or three velocity dimensions, the basis is a TensorProductBasis, and the coefficients are an array of VD dimensions. The mass matrix is the Kronecker product of the one-dimensional mass matrices. It is never formed: a solve is one one-dimensional solve per axis.

sdist2 = SplineDistribution(1, 2, 9, 3, (-4.0, 4.0))
size(sdist2.coefficients)
(10, 10)

Particles that leave the domain

The velocity domain of a spline is bounded, and the velocities of the particles are not. A particle outside the domain contributes nothing to the projection, so its weight would be lost from $f_s$. The projection therefore throws a DomainError for such a particle. Two remedies exist: a larger domain, or oversized end cells. The argument length_big_cell > 0 appends one cell of that width at each end of the domain:

wide = SplineDistribution(1, 1, 41, 4, (-10.0, 10.0), 5.0)
domain(wide.basis)
-15.0 .. 15.0

The boundary condition

The boundary condition decides which polynomials the basis reproduces. The moments $\int v^k f_s \, dv$ of the projection of a particle distribution agree with the particle moments $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha^k$ only where $v^k$ is in the span of the basis. polynomial_reproduction reports the largest such degree:

[bc => polynomial_reproduction(SplineDistribution(1, 1, 41, 4, (-10.0, 10.0), 0, bc).basis)
 for bc in (Free(), Periodic(), Dirichlet())]
3-element Vector{Pair{A, Int64} where A}:
      Free() => 3
  Periodic() => 0
 Dirichlet() => -1

Free(), the default, is the unconstrained clamped basis. It is the only one of the three that reproduces $1$, $v$ and $v^2$, so it is the only one on which the mass, the momentum and the energy of $f_s$ are those of the particles. check_conservation_basis throws where a basis does not reproduce a given degree.

Grids in phase space

A GridDistribution holds the values $f_{ij} = f(x_i, v_j)$ at the nodes of an $n_x \times n_v$ grid. The $x$ axis is periodic, so its right end is not stored. The $v$ axis is bounded, and both of its ends are nodes:

using VlasovMethods

grid = GridDistribution(8, 65, (0.0, 2π), (-6.0, 6.0))
grid.values .= (1 .+ 0.1 .* cos.(grid.x)) .* exp.(-grid.v' .^ 2 ./ 2) ./ sqrt(2π)
(step(grid.x), step(grid.v), grid(π / 4, 0.0))
(0.7853981633974483, 0.1875, 0.42715175957882057)

grid(x, v) interpolates bilinearly. It is periodic in $x$ and zero outside the velocity range. velocity_moments integrates over $v$ at each $x$-node and returns the density, the momentum and the kinetic energy density:

velocity_moments(grid).density

The entropy

The collision operators dissipate the entropy of the spline projection,

\[S_h = \int_\Omega f_s \log |f_s| \, dv .\]

A CollisionEntropy holds the spline distribution of a collision operator, and calling it evaluates $S_h$ on the Gauß–Legendre nodes of the basis:

entropy = CollisionEntropy(sdist)
entropy()
-1.4189384693246003

The projected unit Maxwellian has the entropy $-\tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \log 2\pi) \approx -1.4189$. The collision operators take a CollisionEntropy as their second argument, and use its spline distribution for every projection.
