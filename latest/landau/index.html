The Landau collision operator

The package implements the two particle methods of the Landau manuscript for two velocity dimensions.

  • Landau(dist, ent; γ = -3, ν = 1.0) is the appendix method. Its vector field is the least-squares solution of a system in the matrices $\mathbb{K}$ of compute_K!, with the right-hand side $\mathbb{L}$ of compute_L! times $\mathbb{L}_k$ of compute_J!. Picard_iterate_Landau_nls! integrates it in time with the implicit midpoint rule.
  • PairwiseLandau is the particle-pair method of the main text. Its vector field is the product of the matrix $\mathbb{G}$ of apply_G! with the gradient of the entropy of entropy_gradient!. discrete_gradient_step! integrates it in time with a discrete gradient, by default the GonzalezGradient.

Both methods take the kernel $U$ of kernel, with the exponent γ.

The appendix method

VlasovMethods.kernel — Function
kernel(v_α, v_β, landau::Landau)

The Landau collision kernel

\[U(u) = |u|^{\gamma + 2} \left( \mathbb{I} - \frac{u \otimes u}{|u|^2} \right) , \qquad u = v_\alpha - v_\beta ,\]

with the exponent $\gamma$ = landau.γ: the orthogonal projector onto the complement of $u$, scaled by $|u|^{\gamma + 2}$. $\gamma = -3$, the default of Landau, is the Coulomb kernel $|u|^{-1} (\mathbb{I} - u \otimes u / |u|^2)$, and $\gamma = 0$ is the kernel of Maxwell molecules, $|u|^2 \mathbb{I} - u \otimes u$. Only the first two components of the velocities are read.

The properties the conservation proofs rest on hold for the expression implemented here: $U$ is symmetric, $U(v_\alpha, v_\beta) = U(v_\beta, v_\alpha)$, and $U u = 0$ up to rounding.

The coincident point

At $u = 0$ the kernel is the zero matrix, for every $\gamma$. For $\gamma > -2$ zero is the limit of $U$; for $\gamma \le -2$ $U$ has no limit there. The value does not enter $\mathbb{L}$ of compute_L!, the one caller that reaches $u = 0$: there $U(v, v')$ multiplies the gradient differences $\nabla \varphi_i(v) - \nabla \varphi_i(v')$, which vanish at $v = v'$, so the coincident term of the quadrature sum is $0^{\mathsf T} U \, 0$, and any finite value at $u = 0$ gives the same $\mathbb{L}$. The two factors of the double integral share their Gauß-Legendre nodes, and the error of $\mathbb{L}$ falls under cell refinement: for the projected unit Gaussian on $[-4, 4]^2$ and $\gamma = -3$, measured in the relative Frobenius norm against a rule of eight more nodes per cell on the same basis, at an order of about 1.8 for quadratic splines on 4 to 16 cells, and for cubic splines of about 2.6 from 4 to 8 cells and 1.7 from 8 to 16.

The exponent

Landau accepts a finite $\gamma > -6$ and throws an ArgumentError otherwise. In two dimensions both gradient differences are of order $|u|$ near $u = 0$ for splines of degree at least 2, whose gradients are Lipschitz, so the integrand of $\mathbb{L}$ behaves as $|u|^{\gamma + 4}$ and $\int r^{\gamma + 5} \, dr$ converges at $r = 0$ exactly for $\gamma > -6$. For linear splines the gradients jump across the cell edges: the gradient differences are of order one on the pairs that straddle an edge, a fraction of order $|u|$ of the pairs at distance $|u|$, so the integral of the continuum converges exactly for $\gamma > -5$. The quadrature sum of compute_L! is finite for every accepted $\gamma$.

VlasovMethods.compute_K! — Function
compute_K!(K1, K2, v_array, sdist, landau)

The matrix $\mathbb{K}_{k,\alpha} = w_\alpha \, \nabla \varphi_k(v_\alpha)$ of the Landau manuscript's appendix, split into its two velocity components.

Both matrices are cleared first

They are cache arrays, allocated once and written only at the entries whose basis functions the current particle positions overlap. Without the fill! the entries written on earlier calls survive: as particles move between cells, $\mathbb{K}$ accumulates spurious nonzeros from previous Picard iterations and previous time steps, and $\mathbb{K}^{+}$ is then the pseudo-inverse of a matrix that is not $w_\alpha \nabla \varphi_k(v_\alpha)$.

Every index is bounded per component before being flattened. Testing only the flat index does not bound the components: with i = 0, j = 3, M = 10 the flat index (j-1)M + i = 20 passes a 1 ≤ k ≤ M² test and decodes to (10, 2), so an out-of-support contribution is aliased onto an unrelated basis function instead of being discarded. Wrapping goes through basis_index, which is periodic-aware, so the gradient is evaluated with the same wrap as the spline it differentiates.

The evaluation buffers are those of landau.cache[T], with T the element type of v_array, so a call allocates nothing. They depend only on the order of the basis, and no quadrature is read, so the result is exact on any sdist of the cache's basis order.

VlasovMethods.compute_L! — Function
compute_L!(L, sdist, landau)

The symmetric matrix

\[\mathbb{L}_{ij} = \frac{1}{2} \int_\Omega \! \int_\Omega \big( \nabla \varphi_i(v) - \nabla \varphi_i(v') \big) \cdot |f_s(v)| \, U(v,v') \, |f_s(v')| \cdot \big( \nabla \varphi_j(v) - \nabla \varphi_j(v') \big) \, dv \, dv'\]

of the Landau manuscript's eq:discrete-landau-matrix, from its appendix bracket eq:landau_bracket_modified with the mobility $M(f) = |f|$. $U$ is the kernel of the exponent $\gamma$ = landau.γ, the Coulomb kernel for the default $\gamma = -3$.

Mobility

The bracket allows any mobility $M(f) \ge 0$. With $M(f) = f$, $\mathbb{L}$ loses positive semi-definiteness where $f_s$ changes sign, and with it the discrete H-theorem. With $|f_s|$, every term of the quadrature sum is a non-negative weight times a positive semi-definite form, so $\mathbb{L}$ is symmetric and positive semi-definite for every finite $f_s$. Where the basis functions sum to one, $\mathbb{L} \mathbb{1} = 0$ as well, because the gradient of their sum vanishes. A non-finite $f_s$ is not checked here: collisional_vectorfield! calls compute_J! first, which throws.

Method

Expanding the two differences gives four terms. With $U$ symmetric in its arguments and in its indices, relabelling $v \leftrightarrow v'$ shows the first and fourth are equal and the second and third are equal, so the factor of one half cancels and

\[\mathbb{L} = \sum_{c,d} D^c \operatorname{diag}(s \odot A^{cd}) (D^d)^{\mathsf T} - \sum_{c,d} \big( D^c \operatorname{diag}(s) \big) \, U^{cd} \, \big( D^d \operatorname{diag}(s) \big)^{\mathsf T} , \qquad A^{cd}_a = \sum_b s_b \, U^{cd}_{ab} ,\]

with $s_a = w_a |f_s(v_a)|$ and $D^c$ the gradient tables above. This is an identity, not an approximation: the same quadrature sum, rearranged.

Both terms are products of sparse $M \times Q$ factors, which hold a few stored entries per grid point. The first is a single pass over the grid; the second is the genuinely coupled one, and its $Q \times Q$ kernel is built and consumed in row blocks of _LANDAU_CHUNK = 256 rows, or Q where Q < 256, so that nothing of that size is ever held.

Buffers

Every array of the assembly is a buffer of landau.cache[T], the LandauCache of the element type of sdist, and the gradient tables are the ones that LandauCache builds once from its own spline distribution, so a call allocates nothing. An sdist whose quadrature is not that same object raises an ArgumentError; collisional_vectorfield! passes the cache's distribution itself, and similar keeps the quadrature.

$U^{cd}$ is symmetric in $(c,d)$, so each block is swept once and its three independent components serve all four contractions. The whole assembly therefore costs $O(Q^2)$ kernel evaluations, two sweeps of the grid pairs in total: one for $A^{cd}$ and one for the blocks.

VlasovMethods.compute_J! — Method
compute_J!(J, sdist, landau::Landau)

The vector

\[\mathbb{L}_k = \sum_i \mathbb{M}^{-1}_{ik} \int_\Omega \varphi_i(v) \left[ 1 + \log \Big| \sum_j f_j \varphi_j(v) \Big| \right] dv\]

of the Landau manuscript's eq:defn_Lk — the $L^2$ projection of $1 + \log |f_s|$ onto the spline basis: $\mathbb{M}^{-1}$ times the gradient of the entropy $S_h = \int f_s \log |f_s| \, dv$ of eq:disc_entropy with respect to the coefficients of $f_s$.

$\log |f_s|$ is defined where the $L^2$ projection of a particle distribution undershoots below zero. A quadrature node where $f_s$ is zero or not finite throws a DomainError.

Written as a projection rather than as one quadrature per coefficient. The integrand is sampled once on the tensor-product Gauß-Legendre grid the basis already carries and contracted against the one-dimensional tabulations, so the cost is the grid size rather than the grid size times the number of coefficients. The quadrature is the one the mass matrix is assembled on, so the two halves of eq:defn_Lk are computed over the same domain.

The sample, the evaluation buffers and the projection's workspace are those of landau.cache[T], the LandauCache of the element type of sdist, so a call allocates nothing. An sdist whose quadrature is not that of the cache's spline distribution raises an ArgumentError, as in compute_L!.

VlasovMethods.Picard_iterate_Landau_nls! — Function
Picard_iterate_Landau_nls!(landau, Δt, ti, t, v_prev, v_prev_2, rhs_prev; abstol,
    max_iters = 100)

Take one implicit-midpoint step of the Landau model from the velocities v_prev by a Picard iteration to the tolerance abstol, write the accepted velocities into the particles of landau.dist, and return (iterations = …, residual = …).

From the guess v₀, iteration k = 0, 1, … evaluates the vector field f_k at the midpoint (v_k + v_prev)/2 and the residual $r_k = v_k - (v_{prev} + Δt \, f_k)$. The first iterate with $\|r_k\|_∞ ≤$ abstol is accepted: the particles take v_prev + Δt f_k, so that the accepted velocities and the stored vector field f_k belong together. Otherwise v_{k+1} = v_prev + Δt f_k. iterations is k + 1, the number of vector-field evaluations, and residual is $\|r_k\|_∞$. The guess of the first three steps evaluates the vector field too; those evaluations are not counted.

abstol must be finite and positive and max_iters at least 1, or an ArgumentError is thrown before any vector field is evaluated. When no iterate meets abstol within max_iters evaluations, an ErrorException is thrown that names the number of evaluations and the last residual, and neither the particles nor rhs_prev are written. A residual that is not finite never meets abstol, and an abstol below the rounding error of the residual, about the machine epsilon of the velocities' element type times their size, may never be met. An exception of the vector field passes through: the projection throws a DomainError once an iterate moves particles out of the velocity domain, which can happen before max_iters evaluations.

ti counts the steps from 1, and t is the time of the new velocities. From the fourth step on, the guess is the Hermite extrapolation from v_prev_2 and v_prev with the vector fields in rhs_prev[:, :, 2] and rhs_prev[:, :, 1]; the first three steps (ti < 4) take it from a midpoint extrapolation of the vector field. On return, rhs_prev[:, :, 1] holds the accepted vector field f_k and rhs_prev[:, :, 2] the one it held before.

The residual $\|r_k\|_∞$ of each iterate is sent as the @debug message "Landau Picard residual" with the keyword iteration = k; nothing is written to stdout.

Allocations

From the fourth step on, a call allocates at most iterations times the bytes of one vector-field evaluation, plus 1 KiB. A vector-field evaluation allocates no more than its two least-squares solves K1 \ LJ and K2 \ LJ where the number of particles is not the number of basis functions (Np ≠ M); for a square K (Np == M) it takes the pivoted QR, where \ takes LU, and that bound does not hold. The guess of the first three steps allocates at most the bytes of its 37 vector-field evaluations plus 200 kB, for the arrays of GeometricIntegrators' extrapolation and the problem it is given. test/integration/landau_solver.jl asserts both bounds.

The particle-pair method

VlasovMethods.PairwiseLandau — Type
PairwiseLandau(dist, ent; ν = 1.0, γ = -3)

The particle-pair Landau scheme of the Landau manuscript's main text: the gradient system

\[\dot{v} = \nu \, \mathbb{G}(v) \, \nabla S_h(v)\]

of eq:landau_sd_gradient_form, with the matrix $\mathbb{G}$ of eq:G_semi-disc,

\[\big( \mathbb{G}(v) \, x \big)_\gamma = - \sum_{\beta \ne \gamma} U(v_\gamma, v_\beta) \left( \frac{w_\beta}{w_\gamma} \, x_\gamma - x_\beta \right) ,\]

and the gradient $\partial S_h / \partial v_\alpha = w_\alpha \sum_k \mathbb{L}_k \nabla \varphi_k(v_\alpha)$ of the entropy $S_h = \int f_s \log |f_s| \, dv$ of the particles' projection. Written out, this is eq:eom_line3,

\[\dot{v}_\gamma = - \nu \sum_{\alpha \ne \gamma} w_\alpha \, U(v_\gamma, v_\alpha) \left( \psi_\gamma - \psi_\alpha \right) , \qquad \psi_\alpha = \sum_k \mathbb{L}_k \nabla \varphi_k(v_\alpha) .\]

$\mathbb{G}$ is symmetric and negative semi-definite, so $S_h$ decreases; $\sum_\gamma w_\gamma \mathbb{G}_{\gamma\sigma} = 0$ conserves the momentum $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha$ and $\sum_\gamma w_\gamma v_\gamma \cdot \mathbb{G}_{\gamma\sigma} = 0$ the energy $\frac{1}{2} \sum_\alpha w_\alpha |v_\alpha|^2$. For distinct velocities that do not all lie on one line the kernel of $\mathbb{G}$ has the dimension $d + 1 = 3$. Only two velocity dimensions are supported.

The model holds a Landau of the same arguments in its field landau, and takes from it the particle distribution, the projection onto the spline distribution of ent, the matrices $\mathbb{K}$ of compute_K! and $\mathbb{L}_k$ of compute_J!, the kernel of the exponent γ, its check of γ and the buffers of its cache. The weights are row 1 of dist.particles.w. $\mathbb{G}$ divides by them, so a weight that is not positive and finite throws an ArgumentError, in the constructor and in each apply_G!, which reads the weights of the distribution as they are at the call.

The particle velocities must be distinct: $U(v, v)$ is not defined, and two particles of equal velocities throw a DomainError. For $\gamma < -2$, where $U$ diverges as $|u| \to 0$, so do two different velocities whose squared distance is below a threshold of the element type T and of $\gamma$, at which $U$ in T stops being finite and not zero: for $\gamma = -3$ a distance below $(2 / \mathtt{floatmax}(T)^{2/3})^{1/2}$, 2.0e-13 in Float32 and 2.5e-103 in Float64. For $\gamma \ge -2$ $U$ stays finite, and only equal velocities throw.

Cost

One product $\mathbb{G}(v) \, x$ of apply_G! costs O(N²) kernel evaluations, one per ordered pair of particles. On the fixture of test/helpers/pairwise_landau.jl widened to a lattice of N particles, quadratic splines on 8 × 8 cells (M = 100, Q = 576), Float64, the minimum of 5 calls after a warm call in a cold process: apply_G! takes 0.36 ms at N = 400 and 5.75 ms at N = 1600, 2.25 ns per pair, at a load of about 10; the gradient $\nabla S_h$ takes 0.08 ms and 0.27 ms, and $S_h$ 0.05 ms and 0.10 ms, at a load of about 11.

The time integrator is discrete_gradient_step!.

VlasovMethods.entropy_gradient! — Function
entropy_gradient!(∇S, v, model::PairwiseLandau)

The gradient $\partial S_h / \partial v_{c,\alpha} = \sum_k \mathbb{K}_c[k, \alpha] \, \mathbb{L}_k$ of the entropy $S_h = \int f_s \log |f_s| \, dv$ of the projection of the particles at the velocities v (2 × N), written into ∇S (2 × N). The projection, $\mathbb{K}$ and $\mathbb{L}_k$ are those of model.landau, with the buffers of its cache of the element type of v; the projection overwrites the coefficients of that cache's spline distribution. v or ∇S of another size than 2 × N, with N the number of particles of the model, throws a DimensionMismatch.

VlasovMethods.apply_G! — Function
apply_G!(y, v, x, model::PairwiseLandau)

The product $y = \mathbb{G}(v) \, x$ of the matrix of eq:G_semi-disc at the velocities v with x, all three 2 × N:

\[y_\gamma = - \sum_{\beta \ne \gamma} U(v_\gamma, v_\beta) \left( \frac{w_\beta}{w_\gamma} \, x_\gamma - x_\beta \right) ,\]

with $U$ the kernel of model.landau and the weights $w$ of row 1 of model.landau.dist.particles.w. The pair $\beta = \gamma$ is skipped by its index. Two particles $\gamma \ne \beta$ of equal velocities throw a DomainError that names both, the smaller index first. For $\gamma < -2$ so do two different velocities whose squared distance is below the threshold of the element type of v at which $U$ stops being finite and not zero. y must not alias v or x. An array of another size than 2 × N, with N the number of particles of the model, throws a DimensionMismatch, and a weight that is not positive and finite an ArgumentError.

The cost is $O(N^2)$ kernel evaluations, one per ordered pair; the PairwiseLandau docstring gives measured times. A call allocates nothing.

collisional_vectorfield!(v̇, v, params, model::PairwiseLandau)

The vector field $\dot{v} = \nu \, \mathbb{G}(v) \, \nabla S_h(v)$ of eq:landau_sd_gradient_form, which is eq:eom_line3, at the velocities v, written into v̇ (both 2 × N). params is not read. VlasovMethods.DiscreteGradient — Type
DiscreteGradient

The discrete gradients $\bar{\nabla} S(v^0, v^1)$ of the entropy that discrete_gradient_step! takes, each a method of discrete_gradient!. A discrete gradient is defined by the two conditions of the Landau manuscript's eq:disc_grad_props: $S(v^1) - S(v^0) = (v^1 - v^0) \cdot \bar{\nabla} S(v^0, v^1)$ and $\bar{\nabla} S(v, v) = \nabla S(v)$. The package implements two of them: the GonzalezGradient, the manuscript's, and the AVFGradient.

VlasovMethods.GonzalezGradient — Type
GonzalezGradient()

The Gonzalez discrete gradient, the Landau manuscript's choice in eq:disc_grad_props:

\[\bar{\nabla} S(v^0, v^1) = \nabla S(\bar{v}) + \frac{S(v^1) - S(v^0) - \Delta v \cdot \nabla S(\bar{v})}{|\Delta v|^2} \, \Delta v , \qquad \bar{v} = \frac{v^0 + v^1}{2} , \quad \Delta v = v^1 - v^0 ,\]

with the dot product and the norm over all 2N entries. It satisfies $S(v^1) - S(v^0) = \Delta v \cdot \bar{\nabla} S$ to rounding. Where the computed $|\Delta v|^2$ is zero it is $\nabla S(\bar{v})$, without a division.

VlasovMethods.AVFGradient — Type
AVFGradient(nq::Integer = 5)

The averaged-vector-field discrete gradient: the mean of the entropy gradient along the segment between the two velocities,

\[\bar{\nabla} S_h(v^0, v^1) = \int_0^1 \nabla S_h\big( (1 - \tau) \, v^0 + \tau \, v^1 \big) \, d\tau ,\]

taken by the nq-point Gauß–Legendre rule on $[0, 1]$,

\[\bar{\nabla} S_h(v^0, v^1) = \sum_{i = 1}^{nq} \omega_i \, \nabla S_h\big( v^0 + \tau_i \, \Delta v \big) , \qquad \Delta v = v^1 - v^0 ,\]

with the nodes $\tau_i$ in increasing order and symmetric about $1/2$ and the weights $\omega_i > 0$ summing to one. The rule integrates $\tau^k$ exactly for $k \le 2nq - 1$, because Gauß–Legendre of nq nodes is exact for polynomials of degree $2nq - 1$; the constructor computes it in Float64 by Newton's method on the Legendre polynomial, once per rule, and converts a node and a weight to the element type of the velocities at each use.

The identity $S_h(v^1) - S_h(v^0) = \Delta v \cdot \bar{\nabla} S_h$ of the Landau manuscript's eq:disc_grad_props holds to the quadrature error and not to rounding, because $S_h$ is not a polynomial in the velocities, and the rounding of the sum does not bound that error. The other condition, $\bar{\nabla} S_h(v, v) = \nabla S_h(v)$, holds bit for bit: at $v^1 = v^0$ a call evaluates $\nabla S_h$ once, at $v^0$. At nq = 1 the single node is $1/2$, so the rule is the midpoint gradient $\nabla S_h \big( (v^0 + v^1)/2 \big)$.

Cost

A call takes nq evaluations of $\nabla S_h$ (one at $v^1 = v^0$) and no $S_h$, where the GonzalezGradient takes one $\nabla S_h$ and two $S_h$ (one $\nabla S_h$ at $v^1 = v^0$); one apply_G! of O(N²) follows either in the step. A call allocates nothing after a warm call: the node velocities go into the buffer v of model.landau.cache[T], the accumulated gradient into ∇̄S, and the gradient of each later node into the buffer v̇.

Errors

An nq below 1 throws an ArgumentError that names it, before any node is computed; nq that is not an integer has no method.

VlasovMethods.discrete_gradient_step! — Function
discrete_gradient_step!(model::PairwiseLandau, Δt; abstol, max_iters = 100,
    gradient = GonzalezGradient())

Take one step of the discrete gradient scheme eq:disc_grad_update_rule of the Landau manuscript,

\[\frac{v^{n+1} - v^n}{\Delta t} = \nu \, \mathbb{G}\!\left( \frac{v^n + v^{n+1}}{2} \right) \bar{\nabla} S_h(v^n, v^{n+1}) ,\]

with $\mathbb{G}$ at the midpoint (eq:midpoint_G) and the discrete gradient gradient, from the velocities $v^n$ of model.landau.dist.particles.v; write the accepted velocities into the particles and return (iterations = …, residual = …).

From $v_0 = v^n$, iteration $k = 0, 1, …$ evaluates $f_k = \nu \, \mathbb{G}(m_k) \, \bar{\nabla} S_h(v^n, v_k)$ with $m_k = (v^n + v_k)/2$, and the residual $r_k = v_k - v^n - \Delta t \, f_k$. The first iterate with $\|r_k\|_\infty \le$ abstol is accepted: the particles take $v^n + \Delta t \, f_k$, and the step returns iterations = k + 1, the number of evaluations of $f$, and residual $= \|r_k\|_\infty$. Otherwise $v_{k+1} = v^n + \Delta t \, f_k$. The first evaluation, at $v_0 = v^n$, takes $\bar{\nabla} S_h(v^n, v^n) = \nabla S_h(v^n)$, the explicit Euler step.

Structure

The accepted state conserves the momentum $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha$ to rounding, because $\sum_\gamma w_\gamma \mathbb{G}_{\gamma\sigma} = 0$. With $\mathbb{G}$ and the iterate at the same midpoint $m_k$, $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha f_\alpha \cdot m_\alpha = 0$, so the energy changes by $-(\Delta t / 2) \sum_\alpha w_\alpha f_\alpha \cdot r_\alpha$, at most $(\Delta t / 2) \, \text{abstol} \sum_\alpha w_\alpha \|f_\alpha\|_1$. The discrete gradient gives $S_h(v^{n+1}) - S_h(v^n) = (v^{n+1} - v^n) \cdot \bar{\nabla} S_h$ (eq:disc_grad_props), and so the discrete H-theorem: $S_h$ does not increase, because $\mathbb{G}$ is negative semi-definite and $\Delta t > 0$. The H-theorem holds for the fixed point of the iteration; the accepted state differs from it by a residual of at most abstol.

The discrete gradients

The keyword gradient takes either rule. GonzalezGradient, the default, satisfies the identity $S_h(v^{n+1}) - S_h(v^n) = (v^{n+1} - v^n) \cdot \bar{\nabla} S_h$ of eq:disc_grad_props to rounding, because it corrects the midpoint gradient by the two entropies and the displacement. AVFGradient averages the entropy gradient along the segment with an nq-point Gauß–Legendre rule, and takes that identity to the quadrature error and not to rounding, because $S_h$ is not a polynomial in the velocities, and the rounding of its sum does not bound that error. The conservation above holds for both rules, because it comes from $\mathbb{G}$ at the midpoint and the weights, not from the gradient. The H-theorem holds for GonzalezGradient to rounding, and for AVFGradient to the quadrature error of its identity, which is not zero: the entropy falls where $\Delta v \cdot \bar{\nabla} S_h$ exceeds that error. The two rules differ in cost, in the section below.

Tolerance

The residual stalls at the rounding of one evaluation, and abstol must lie above that floor, or the step runs to max_iters and throws. On the fixture of test/helpers/pairwise_landau.jl (144 particles on $[-4, 4]^2$, γ = -3), the residuals of the 20th to the 30th evaluation lie between 1.1e-15 and 1.6e-14 in Float64 and between 3.6e-7 and 1.5e-5 in Float32, for Δt from 5e-5 to 8e-3; for γ = 0 at Δt = 5e-5, between 1.1e-14 and 5.3e-14 in Float64 and up to 8.1e-5 in Float32. The iteration contracts by a factor of about 100 per evaluation at Δt = 2e-3 and γ = -3, and more slowly for γ = 0. The manuscript's stall "at about $10^{-11}$" is the same floor in the relative increment $\|v_{k+1} - v_k\| / \|v_{k+1} - v^n\|$.

Errors

Δt, converted to the element type of the velocities, and abstol must be finite and positive and max_iters at least 1, or an ArgumentError that names the argument is thrown before any evaluation: a Float64 Δt that is zero or Inf in Float32 is refused. So is a particle weight that is not positive and finite, with an ArgumentError that names the particle. When no iterate meets abstol within max_iters evaluations, an ErrorException is thrown that names the number of evaluations and the last residual. A residual that is not finite never meets abstol. The exceptions of the vector field pass through: a DomainError for two equal velocities and, for $\gamma < -2$, for two velocities too close, and the projection's DomainError for a velocity outside the domain of the spline basis, which the step evaluates at the accepted state before the particles take it. The AVFGradient's nodes lie strictly inside the segment, so the state they accept can be outside the domain where none of them is. In each case the particles are not written.

Cost

One evaluation of $f$ is one apply_G! of $O(N^2)$ and the gradient it multiplies: one $\nabla S_h$ and, from the second evaluation, two $S_h$ for the GonzalezGradient; one $\nabla S_h$ and, from the second evaluation, nq $\nabla S_h$ and no $S_h$ for the AVFGradient. A step allocates four 2 × N arrays and takes one projection of the accepted state, for either rule, and each $S_h$ of CollisionEntropy allocates: 92 kB on 8 × 8 quadratic cells.
