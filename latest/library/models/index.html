Methods

VlasovMethods.SplittingMethod — Type
SplittingMethod(model, tspan, tstep)

The splitting method of the particle model model over the time span tspan with the step tstep: the GeometricEquations.SODEProblem of the parts of the model's vector field and the step of each part, and the GeometricIntegrators integrator of their Strang composition. The state is a copy of model.distribution.particles.z.

A 1D1V VlasovPoisson model of a ParticleDistribution splits into the advection $\dot{x} = v$ and the acceleration $\dot{v} = E(x)$, each solved exactly. A VlasovWithExternalFields model splits into the drift $\dot{x} = v$, solved exactly, and the Lorentz force, whose step is a Boris push, an approximation of its exact solution.

The fields model, equation and integrator hold the three parts. run! advances the integrator, and GeometricIntegrators.integrate(method.integrator) returns the solution in memory.

VlasovMethods.run! — Method
run!(method::SplittingMethod, h5file)

Advance the splitting method method over its time span, write every state into the HDF5 file h5file, copy the final state into method.model.distribution.particles.z, and return that distribution.

The file holds the dataset z of the size (nd, npart, ntime): z[:, :, s] is the state of the particles at the s-th time of t₀:Δt:t₁, the initial state included. The file is created, or overwritten, and closed on return, also when a step throws.

VlasovMethods.GeometricIntegrator — Type
GeometricIntegrator(model, tspan, tstep[, method])

The method that advances model over the time span tspan with the step tstep as one GeometricEquations.ODEProblem, solved by the GeometricIntegrators method method.

The fields model, equation and integrator hold the model, the problem and the integrator. Each model defines its own constructor, with its own state and default method; the docstrings of the constructors below list them. run! advances the integrator.

VlasovMethods.run! — Method
run!(int::GeometricIntegrator, h5file)

Advance the method int over its time span, write every state into the HDF5 file h5file, copy the final state into the distribution of the model, and return that distribution.

The file holds the dataset z of the size (n, ntime), one column per state, and the dataset t of the ntime times of t₀:Δt:t₁, the initial time included. The file is created, or overwritten, and closed on return, also when a step throws. A progress bar is shown for a run of more than five seconds.

Vlasov–Poisson

VlasovMethods.VlasovHamiltonian — Type
VlasovHamiltonian(model)

The energy of the Vlasov–Poisson model model, as an invariant h(t, q, params),

\[h(t, q, w) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_i w_i v_i^2 + \frac{1}{2}\int (\partial_x\phi)^2 \,\mathrm{d}x ,\]

where $\phi$ solves the Poisson problem for a fresh deposit of the positions in q with the weights params.w. The call leaves that deposit in model.potential. Each acceleration substep deposits its own state before it reads the field, so this does not change a trajectory.

The field term is the Gauss quadrature of $(\partial_x\phi)^2$ on the basis of the potential, tabulated once by the constructor, so a call allocates nothing. Its terms are non-negative, so the sum keeps their relative accuracy also for a small field.

source
VlasovMethods.VlasovPoisson — Type
VlasovPoisson(dist::DistributionFunction, potential::Potential)

The Vlasov–Poisson model of one species with the distribution dist, whose field is the solution of potential.

For a ParticleDistribution or a SplineDistribution, the field is the potential's solve for the deposit of the distribution, and potential is on a B-spline basis.

For a 1D1V GridDistribution, the field is $\phi = G \rho$ with $\rho_i = h_v \sum_j f_{ij}$, and the constructor assembles the $n_x \times n_x$ Green's matrix $G$ of the potential's backend once. GeometricIntegrator(model, tspan, tstep, method) steps the model. The constructor throws:

  • ArgumentError unless the potential's solver is a PoissonSolverFFT or a PoissonSolverMatrixFree;
  • DimensionMismatch unless the potential has one degree of freedom per x-node of dist;
  • ArgumentError unless the potential's x-nodes are dist.x, and unless dist has at least three nodes in each direction.

With the default PeriodicVelocityClosure, the grid scheme is periodic in v: it is valid only while f vanishes to eps(T)·maximum(f) at both ends of the velocity range. The keyword closure = BoundedVelocityClosure() of GeometricIntegrator bounds the velocity range instead (see BoundedVelocityClosure).

VlasovMethods.sodeensemble — Method
sodeensemble(model, members, tspan, tstep)

The training data of model as a GeometricEquations.SODEEnsemble, one sample per ParticleDistribution in members.

The initial condition of a member m is (q = copy(m.particles.z),), and its parameters are its own weights, (w = copy(m.particles.w),), because an importance-sampled draw gives each member different weights. Both are copies, so a later change to m does not reach the problem. The invariant h is the VlasovHamiltonian of model.

GeometricIntegrators.integrate(ens, Strang()) solves every member into one EnsembleSolution, which h5save and h5load round-trip through the HDF5 extension of GeometricSolutions. The invariant is evaluated along a stored solution once per saved state, the initial state included, with

compute_invariant(sol.t, sol[i][:q], parameters(ens)[i], invariants(equation(ens)).h)

integrate itself evaluates no invariant.

All members share the potential and the work buffer of model. Solve them one after another, as integrate does: members solved concurrently read each other's fields, with no error.

An empty member list, and a member whose weights and state have different particle counts, throw an ArgumentError. The model and the members have one element type T; a member of another element type raises a MethodError.

VlasovMethods.BoundedVelocityClosure — Type
BoundedVelocityClosure()

The bounded closure of the velocity grid, with zero inflow. The unknowns are the nodes $j = 2, …, n_v - 1$, and $f$ and the Hamiltonian are read as zero on and beyond the end nodes $j = 1$ and $j = n_v$, with $\chi$ the indicator of the unknowns. The field is the periodic bracket of the cut data, read at the unknowns,

\[\dot{f} = \chi \circ [P h, \chi \circ f] , \qquad P h = \chi \circ (\phi \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \tfrac{v^2}{2}) , \qquad \phi = G \rho , \qquad \rho_i = h_v \sum_{j=2}^{n_v-1} f_{ij} ,\]

and zero at the end nodes. On grid functions that vanish on and beyond the end nodes, Arakawa's form $T(a, b, c) = \langle a, [b, c] \rangle$ is totally antisymmetric, so $\dot{f}$ is the field of the cyclic average of $T$, and $\langle P h, \dot{f} \rangle = 0$: the discrete energy $H = h_x h_v \sum_i \sum_{j=2}^{n_v-1} f_{ij} v_j^2 / 2 + (h_x / 2) \rho^\mathsf{T} G \rho$ is kept by the field, and by the implicit midpoint rule. The mass is not kept: its rate is the flux $-h_x h_v \sum_i \sum_{j \in \{1, n_v\}} [P h, \chi \circ f]_{ij}$ through the end nodes. Where $f$ vanishes on the two outermost nodes at each end, $j \in \{1, 2, n_v - 1, n_v\}$, the two closures give the same field.

VlasovMethods.GeometricIntegrator — Type
GeometricIntegrator(model::VlasovPoisson{1, 1, <:GridDistribution}, tspan, tstep,
                    method = Integrators.ImplicitMidpoint();
                    closure::VelocityClosure = PeriodicVelocityClosure())

The GeometricIntegrator that steps the grid 1D1V Vlasov–Poisson model model over tspan with step tstep, in the state vec(model.distribution.values).

method is any GeometricIntegrators method for an ODEProblem. ImplicitMidpoint() keeps $H$; RK4() is the one scripts/landau_damping_1d1v.jl uses, because the implicit midpoint's dense Newton Jacobian is too large on that script's $n_v$. The scheme runs in Float32 and Float64; FFTW has no Float16 plan.

closure is the closure of the velocity grid. With PeriodicVelocityClosure, the default, the vector field is _grid_1d1v_rhs!. With BoundedVelocityClosure, the vector field is _grid_1d1v_bounded_rhs!, and the initial state is vec(model.distribution.values) with its columns $1$ and $n_v$ set to zero; model.distribution is not changed until run! writes the states back. A closure of another type throws a TypeError.

VlasovMethods.PeriodicVelocityClosure — Type
PeriodicVelocityClosure()

The periodic closure of the velocity grid, the default: the Arakawa bracket wraps the velocity direction, so the node beyond $v_b$ is $v_a$. The unknowns are all $n_v$ nodes. A run is valid only while $f$ vanishes at both ends of the velocity range.

VlasovMethods._green_matrix — Method
_green_matrix(dist::GridDistribution, potential::Potential)

The $n_x \times n_x$ Green's matrix $G$ of potential's backend on the grid of dist, which gives the field of a density in one product:

\[\rho_i = h_v \sum_j f_{ij} , \qquad \phi = G \rho .\]

It solves the backend for the $n_x$ unit vectors and symmetrises the result. The symmetry makes $\partial H / \partial f = h_x h_v h$ exact, so the discrete Hamiltonian is quadratic in $f$ and an implicit midpoint step conserves it. A spline basis is refused: its density at the nodes is not a Galerkin load vector. PoissonSolverMatrixFree bounds its residual, not its error, so in Float32 on a fine grid G is less accurate than $eps(Float32)$. The errors are listed under VlasovPoisson.

source
VlasovMethods._grid_1d1v_bounded_rhs! — Method
_grid_1d1v_bounded_rhs!(ḟ, t, f, params)

The right-hand side of the grid 1D1V Vlasov–Poisson model with the BoundedVelocityClosure, as the vector field of a GeometricEquations.ODEProblem:

\[\dot{f} = \chi \circ [P h, \chi \circ f] , \qquad P h = \chi \circ (\phi \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes \tfrac{v^2}{2}) , \qquad \phi = G \rho , \qquad \rho_i = h_v \sum_{j=2}^{n_v-1} f_{ij} ,\]

with $\chi$ the indicator of the unknowns $j = 2, …, n_v - 1$ and $[\cdot, \cdot]$ the doubly periodic bracket of _grid_1d1v_rhs!. It writes zero in columns $1$ and $n_v$ of ḟ, and reads no value of f in those columns: it copies the unknowns of f into the buffer g of the cache, whose end columns are zero, so a non-finite value there does not reach ḟ.

params is that of _grid_1d1v_rhs!, and its cache holds g too. A state of the element type T of the buffers allocates nothing; a state of another element type, such as the ForwardDiff.Dual numbers of the implicit midpoint's Jacobian, takes the buffers of its own element type from params.cache. It throws the errors of _grid_1d1v_rhs!, with its messages, before it writes: a DimensionMismatch when f or ḟ does not hold $n_x n_v$ values, and an ArgumentError when ḟ shares memory with f. At $n_v = 3$ the one unknown row has no velocity neighbour among the unknowns, and the field is zero.

source
VlasovMethods._grid_1d1v_rhs! — Method
_grid_1d1v_rhs!(ḟ, t, f, params)

Arakawa's discretisation of the 1D1V Vlasov–Poisson right-hand side, as the vector field of a GeometricEquations.ODEProblem:

\[\dot{f} = - [f, h] , \qquad h_{ij} = \frac{v_j^2}{2} + \phi_i , \qquad \phi = G \rho , \qquad \rho_i = h_v \sum_j f_{ij} ,\]

for a charge of $+1$, with G from _green_matrix. params holds the bracket arakawa, the velocity nodes v and G; the state f is vec(dist.values). The bracket is doubly periodic, so a distribution that reaches the ends of the velocity range wraps mass from $v_b$ to $v_a$ (see VlasovPoisson).

params also holds buffers in the element type T of the distribution: kinetic, the $v_j^2 / 2$, and ρ, ϕ and h. A state and a derivative of element type T take a method that writes into these buffers and allocates nothing. A state of another element type, such as the ForwardDiff.Dual numbers of the implicit midpoint's Jacobian, takes the buffers ρ, ϕ and h of its own element type from params.cache, a CacheDict whose parent holds the buffers of T: the first call of a type builds them, and a later call allocates nothing. Either method throws a DimensionMismatch before it writes any buffer when f or ḟ does not hold $n_x n_v$ values, and an ArgumentError when ḟ shares memory with f. A params without these buffers takes a method that allocates its intermediate arrays. The buffers belong to one task: two concurrent calls on one params are unsafe, because they share them.

External electromagnetic fields

VlasovMethods.UniformField — Type
UniformField(E, B)

A constant electric and magnetic field, E and B in Cartesian components, stored as SVector{3,T} with T the promoted element type of the two arguments. It is constant in x and in t, so it is the field of the analytic cases: a gyro-orbit at E = 0, and a constant acceleration along B at E ∥ B.

E and B are two vectors of length three, or two SVector{3}s; a length other than three throws the DimensionMismatch of SVector{3}. A constant field of Int coordinates is written UniformField(SVector(0, 0, 0), SVector(0, 0, 0)).

source
VlasovMethods.VlasovWithExternalFields — Type
VlasovWithExternalFields(dist, field; charge = one(T), mass = one(T))

A VlasovModel of one particle species in an external electromagnetic field: the particles of dist, a ParticleDistribution{T,3,3}, pushed by field. charge and mass are the species' charge and mass, in units in which the equation of motion reads ẍ = (q/m)(E + ẋ × B), and both are stored as T. The constructor throws an ArgumentError unless the converted mass is positive. An empty particle list is a vacuum run and not an error: the model, its SplittingMethod and run! work over the empty state.

The field is any value that defines electric_field and magnetic_field for an SVector{3} coordinate; the model puts no bound on the type of its field parameter, so the two functions are the whole contract. UniformField is the field type this package ships, and the ElectromagneticFields extension adds the two methods for that package's FieldFunctions of a Cartesian chart — FieldFunctions(ABCEquilibrium(a, b, c)) is passed to the constructor as it is. In a curvilinear chart the components are not Cartesian, so the extension defines no method and the first kick raises its MethodError.

The method is SplittingMethod(model, tspan, tstep): a Strang splitting of the drift ẋ = v and the Lorentz force v̇ = (q/m)(E + v × B), with the rotational part of the force a Boris push. One step is a half drift, two half Boris pushes with the field evaluated at the step's start, and a half drift. GeometricIntegrators.integrate(method.integrator) and run!(method, h5file) serve it as they serve the Vlasov–Poisson model.

source
VlasovMethods.electric_field — Function
electric_field(field, t, x)
magnetic_field(field, t, x)

The electric and magnetic field of field at the time t and the position x, each an SVector{3,T} in Cartesian components, with T the element type of x.

The two functions are the interface between a VlasovWithExternalFields model and the field its particles move in. The model calls them once per particle and per kick, with x the particle's position and t the time the kick starts at, and a field type defines one method of each. There is no abstract field type and no fallback method, so a field that defines neither raises its MethodError at the first kick inside integrate.

UniformField is the one field type in this package. The ElectromagneticFields extension adds methods for that package's FieldFunctions of a Cartesian chart; see VlasovWithExternalFields.

magnetic_field(field, t, x)

VlasovMethods.LenardBernstein — Type
LenardBernstein(dist, ent; ν = 1.0)

The Lenard–Bernstein collision operator on the particles of dist, with the entropy ent, a CollisionEntropy of a SplineDistribution, and the collision frequency ν. The particle velocities move with

\[\dot{v}_\alpha = - \nu \left( \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} + v_\alpha \right) ,\]

where $f_s$ is the projection of the particles onto the spline distribution of ent. This is the conservative operator of ConservativeLenardBernstein with the coefficients fixed at $A_1 = 0$ and $A_2 = 1$: it conserves the mass, but not the momentum or the energy, and it relaxes every distribution to the unit Maxwellian.

GeometricIntegrator steps the model of a 1D1V distribution, with RK438() by default. The model keeps one cache of work arrays per element type of the state, so an evaluation of the right-hand side allocates nothing. A cache belongs to one task.

source
VlasovMethods.LB_rhs! — Method

The plain Lenard-Bernstein right-hand side,

\[\dot{v}_\alpha = - \nu \left( \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} + v_\alpha \right) ,\]

i.e. the conservative operator of eq:cons_LB with the coefficients fixed at $A_1 = 0$, $A_2 = 1$. It conserves mass but not momentum or energy — that is the whole point of the conservative variant.

The division by `f_s`

The particles move with the advection coefficient, not with the collisional flux $F[f_s] = -\nu (f_s' + v f_s)$. The two differ by a factor of $f_s(v_\alpha)$ and have the same equilibrium $f_s'/f_s = -v$, but the flux barely advects the particles in the tails, where $f_s$ is small, and so gives a wrong transient.

source
VlasovMethods.ConservativeLenardBernstein — Type
ConservativeLenardBernstein(dist, ent; ν = 1.0)

The conservative Lenard–Bernstein collision operator of the PD-LB manuscript on the particles of dist, with the entropy ent, a CollisionEntropy of a SplineDistribution, and the collision frequency ν. The particle velocities move with

\[\dot{v}_\alpha = - \nu \left( \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} + A_1 + A_2 v_\alpha \right) ,\]

eq:velocity_ode of the manuscript, where $f_s$ is the projection of the particles onto the spline distribution of ent, and the coefficients $A_1$ and $A_2$ of compute_coefficients are solved at every evaluation from the conservation of the momentum and the energy. Every Maxwellian is an equilibrium.

The particle momentum $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha$ and energy $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha^2$ are conserved by the right-hand side on any basis. The moments of $f_s$ agree with them only where the basis reproduces $1$, $v$ and $v^2$, which the default Free() boundary condition does; the constructor warns once on a basis that does not.

The right-hand side divides by $f_s(v_\alpha)$, so it throws a DomainError where the projection is not positive at a particle.

GeometricIntegrator steps the model of a distribution of one velocity dimension, with ImplicitMidpoint() by default.

VlasovMethods.CLB_rhs_GI! — Method

The conservative Lenard-Bernstein right-hand side,

\[\dot{v}_\alpha = - \nu \left( \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} + A_1 + A_2 v_\alpha \right) ,\]

which is eq:velocity_ode of the Lenard-Bernstein manuscript.

The sign

The particles move with the transport velocity $a = -\nu (f'/f + A_1 + A_2 v)$ of the continuity equation $\partial_t f + \partial_v (a f) = 0$. The check is the H-theorem: with $F = f' + (A_1 + A_2 v) f$, the constraints $\int F \, dv = \int v F \, dv = 0$ give $\frac{d}{dt} \int f \log f \, dv = -\nu \int F^2 / f \, dv \le 0$ for $f > 0$. A centred Maxwellian of any width $\sigma$ gives $A_1 = 0$ and $A_2 = \sigma^{-2}$, so $a = 0$: the operator conserves the energy, and every Maxwellian is an equilibrium.

VlasovMethods.compute_coefficients — Method
compute_coefficients(distribution, particle_dist, vp[, values])

The coefficients $A_1$, $A_2$ of the conservative Lenard-Bernstein drift, from the requirement that the operator conserve momentum and energy:

\[\begin{pmatrix} n_h & n_h u_h \\ n_h u_h & n_h \varepsilon_h \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} A_1 \\ A_2 \end{pmatrix} = - \sum_\alpha w_\alpha \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ v_\alpha \end{pmatrix} \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} , \qquad \begin{aligned} n_h &= \textstyle\sum_\alpha w_\alpha , \\ n_h u_h &= \textstyle\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha , \\ n_h \varepsilon_h &= \textstyle\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha^2 , \end{aligned}\]

solved by Cramer's rule. This is eq:coefficient_lin_system of the Lenard-Bernstein manuscript.

values is the buffer of the point evaluations of $f_s$ and $f_s'$: local_width entries of an element type that holds the evaluations, such as the values of the model's cache of the element type of vp. With it the call allocates nothing; without it, the call allocates one. A buffer belongs to one task.

The sums run in the element type of vp, or in the wider type of values where it is wider, with compensated summation, so that a Float32 state with Float32 buffers returns Float32 coefficients whose sums are exact to a few ulp of that type.

The particle weights belong in every sum

They were absent from all five of them. Both the matrix and the right-hand side are linear in $w$, so for uniform weights the factor cancels and $A_1, A_2$ come out unchanged — which is why this never showed up: every initialiser in examples/ sets w = 1/npart. What the earlier version actually annihilated was $\sum_\alpha \dot{v}_\alpha$ rather than $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha \dot{v}_\alpha$, so with the non-uniform weights the importance-sampling initialiser produces, momentum and energy conservation were lost.

VlasovMethods.RescaledConservativeLenardBernstein — Type
RescaledConservativeLenardBernstein(dist, ent; ν = 1.0)

The conservative Lenard–Bernstein collision operator of ConservativeLenardBernstein in the rescaled parametrisation, in which the coefficient of $v$ is one:

\[\dot{v}_\alpha = - \nu \left( A_1 \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} + A_2 + v_\alpha \right) ,\]

with the coefficients of compute_coefficients_rclb. It is the conservative operator with the time rescaled by $1/A_2$ of the conservative coefficients, one factor for all particles, so it has the same trajectories at another speed. The factor approximates $\varepsilon_h - u_h^2$, and equals it where $u_h B_1 - B_2 = n$, with $B_1$ and $B_2$ the particle sums of $f_s'/f_s$ and $v f_s'/f_s$, whose continuous values are $0$ and $-n$. The arguments, the conservation properties and the errors are those of ConservativeLenardBernstein, but the constructor does not warn on a basis that does not reproduce $1$, $v$ and $v^2$.

GeometricIntegrator steps the model of a distribution of one velocity dimension, with ImplicitMidpoint() by default.

source
VlasovMethods.compute_coefficients_rclb — Method
compute_coefficients_rclb(distribution, particle_dist, vp[, values])

The coefficients of the rescaled parametrisation, in which the coefficient of $v$ is normalised to one:

\[\dot{v}_\alpha = - \nu \left( A_1 \frac{f_s'(v_\alpha)}{f_s(v_\alpha)} + A_2 + v_\alpha \right) .\]

This is the conservative operator with time rescaled by $\varepsilon_h - u_h^2$, so that $A_1^{\mathrm{resc}} = 1 / A_2^{\mathrm{cons}} = \varepsilon_h - u_h^2$ and $A_2^{\mathrm{resc}} = A_1^{\mathrm{cons}} / A_2^{\mathrm{cons}} = -u_h$. It is not the fixed-coefficient cumulant-scaling experiment of the manuscript appendix, which sets the coefficients by hand rather than solving for them.

The particle weights appear in every sum, for the reason given at compute_coefficients, and values is the buffer of the point evaluations as there.

VlasovMethods.DoughertyBracket — Type
DoughertyBracket()

The bracket eq:lb_bracket_discrete of the metriplectic Lenard-Bernstein manuscript, with the kernel $\varepsilon_h - u_h^2 + (v - u_h)(v' - u_h)$. Its equation of motion is eq:semidiscrete_equations_of_motion,

\[\dot{v}_\gamma = \nu \left[ - (\varepsilon_h - u_h^2) \, \frac{S_\gamma}{w_\gamma} + \frac{\varepsilon_h - u_h v_\gamma}{n_h} \sum_\beta S_\beta + \frac{v_\gamma - u_h}{n_h} \sum_\beta v_\beta S_\beta \right] ,\]

with $S_\gamma = \partial S_h / \partial v_\gamma$, the moments $n_h = \sum_\beta w_\beta$, $u_h = \sum_\beta w_\beta v_\beta / n_h$ and $\varepsilon_h = \sum_\beta w_\beta v_\beta^2 / n_h$, and the weight $w_\gamma$ of each particle. The default bracket of MetriplecticLenardBernstein.

source
VlasovMethods.JeyakumarBracket — Type
JeyakumarBracket()

The bracket of DoughertyBracket times $n_h / (\varepsilon_h - u_h^2)$. Its equation of motion is eq:semidiscrete_equations_of_motion of the metriplectic Lenard-Bernstein manuscript times the same factor,

\[\dot{v}_\gamma = \nu \left[ - n_h \, \frac{S_\gamma}{w_\gamma} + \frac{\varepsilon_h - u_h v_\gamma}{\varepsilon_h - u_h^2} \sum_\beta S_\beta + \frac{v_\gamma - u_h}{\varepsilon_h - u_h^2} \sum_\beta v_\beta S_\beta \right] ,\]

VlasovMethods.LenardBernsteinBracket — Type
LenardBernsteinBracket

The metric bracket of MetriplecticLenardBernstein: DoughertyBracket or JeyakumarBracket. Both conserve momentum $\sum_\gamma w_\gamma v_\gamma$ and energy $\sum_\gamma w_\gamma v_\gamma^2$ for any entropy gradient, and their right-hand sides differ by the factor $n_h / (\varepsilon_h - u_h^2)$ in every term, so they share their equilibria.

VlasovMethods.MetriplecticLenardBernstein — Type
MetriplecticLenardBernstein(dist, ent; ν = 1.0, bracket = DoughertyBracket())

The metriplectic Lenard-Bernstein collision operator on the particles of dist, with the entropy ent, the collision frequency ν and the metric bracket bracket, a LenardBernsteinBracket.

The right-hand side of either bracket throws a DomainError when the velocities have no spread that the precision resolves: when $\sum_\gamma w_\gamma (v_\gamma - u_h)^2 / n_h \le \epsilon \, \varepsilon_h$ with the machine epsilon $\epsilon$ of the element type, as for one particle or all particles at one velocity, or when the computed $\varepsilon_h - u_h^2$ is not positive. There $\varepsilon_h - u_h^2$ is zero, or a rounding residue of either sign.

VlasovMethods.Picard_iterate_over_particles! — Method
Picard_iterate_over_particles!(v, vn, vn_minus_one, dv_history, ti, t, Δt, abstol, reltol,
    mlb::MetriplecticLenardBernstein; maxiters = 1000)

Solve one implicit-midpoint step of the metriplectic Lenard–Bernstein model from the velocities vn by a Picard iteration, write the solved velocities into v and return v.

ti counts the steps from 1, and t is the time of the guess. From the fourth step on, the guess is the Hermite extrapolation from vn_minus_one and vn with the derivatives in the columns 2 and 1 of dv_history. The first three steps (ti < 4) take the guess from a midpoint extrapolation of the vector field instead. After the solve, column 2 of dv_history takes column 1, and column 1 the vector field at v. abstol, reltol and maxiters are the f_abstol, f_reltol and max_iterations of the SimpleSolvers solve.

The model mlb keeps the solver and its state for the element type of its particles, and the cache of mlb for the element type of vn keeps the work arrays of the vector field. The first call builds the solver, and a call with other options builds it again. A step with ti ≥ 4 allocates nothing when vn has the element type of the particles of mlb; the guess of the first three steps allocates, and a vn of another element type builds a solver on each call.

A solve that does not converge throws an ErrorException with the residual and the iteration count. An exception of the model, such as the DomainError of a particle that leaves the velocity domain, reaches the caller unchanged.

Throws a DimensionMismatch when v and vn differ in length, when vn or vn_minus_one has another length than the particle count of mlb, or when dv_history does not have one row per particle and two columns. Throws an ArgumentError when v shares memory with vn, vn_minus_one or dv_history, or when vn or vn_minus_one shares memory with dv_history. Each check runs before the step writes to any argument.

VlasovMethods.compute_J! — Method
compute_J!(J, g, values, sdist, ::MetriplecticLenardBernstein)

The vector $\mathbb{L}_k = \sum_i \mathbb{M}^{-1}_{ik} \int \varphi_i \, (1 + \log |f_s|) \, dv$ of eq:defn_Lk of the Landau and Lenard-Bernstein metriplectic manuscripts, which is exactly the $L^2$ projection of $1 + \log |f_s|$ onto the basis.

This discretisation projects $1 + \log |f_s|$ onto the spline space and differentiates the projection, where eq:velocity_ode of the Lenard-Bernstein manuscript uses the pointwise ratio $f_s'(v_\alpha)/f_s(v_\alpha)$. The two agree only up to the projection error of the logarithm. That is deliberate and is what makes this a genuine discrete-gradient system: J is $\mathbb{M}^{-1}$ times $\partial S_h / \partial f_i$ for the entropy $S_h = \int f_s \log |f_s| \, dv$ of eq:disc_entropy.

$\log |f_s| = \frac{1}{2} \log (f_s^2)$ is defined where the $L^2$ projection of a particle distribution undershoots below zero. A quadrature node where $f_s$ is zero or not finite throws a DomainError.

g, of the length of the quadrature nodes, and values, of the local width of the basis, are work arrays.

VlasovMethods.compute_dS! — Method
compute_dS!(dS, buf, J, v, sdist, ::MetriplecticLenardBernstein, pdist)

$\partial S_h / \partial v_\alpha = w_\alpha \sum_k \mathbb{L}_k \, \varphi_k'(v_\alpha)$, the Landau manuscript's eq:entropy_derivative.

The weight $w_\alpha$ is read from pdist, which is why the particle distribution is passed in: it equals $1/N$ for every initialiser in examples/ but not for the importance-sampling one, so 1/length(dS) is not a substitute. buf, of the local width of the basis, is a work array.

VlasovMethods.compute_entropy — Method
compute_entropy(f, mlb::MetriplecticLenardBernstein)

$S_h = \int f \log |f| \, dv$, the entropy of eq:disc_entropy for $f = f_s$: the H-functional that decreases, and the quantity the manuscript's entropy figures report.

Integrated on the Gauß-Legendre grid of the basis rather than adaptively, so that it is the same quadrature the discretisation itself uses. A node where f is zero or not finite throws a DomainError, because $\log |f|$ is undefined there.
