Distributions and projections

Distribution functions

VlasovMethods.ParticleDistribution — Type
ParticleDistribution(xdim, vdim, npart; kwargs...)
ParticleDistribution(xdim, vdim, particles::ParticleList)

The distribution function as the Klimontovich measure of $N$ particles,

\[f_p(x, v) = \sum_{\alpha = 1}^{N} w_\alpha \, \delta(x - x_\alpha) \, \delta(v - v_\alpha) ,\]

with xdim position and vdim velocity components per particle. It is a DistributionFunction{DT, xdim, vdim}, beside SplineDistribution and GridDistribution.

The field particles is a ParticleList of ParticleMethods with the variables x, the xdim × N positions, v, the vdim × N velocities, z, the (xdim + vdim) × N phase-space coordinates, positions first, and w, the 1 × N weights. All four are views of one (xdim + vdim + 1) × N matrix, so a write to one of them changes the others.

The first form allocates npart particles of Float64 zeros and passes kwargs to the ParticleList constructor. The second form wraps an existing list, whose element type is the element type of the distribution.

dist = ParticleDistribution(1, 1, 1000)
dist.particles.w .= 1 / 1000

VlasovMethods.SplineDistribution — Type
SplineDistribution(xdim, vdim, nknots, s_order, domain, length_big_cell, bc = Free())

Build a SplineDistribution on nknots equally spaced breakpoints across domain, of spline order s_order, with boundary condition bc on every velocity axis.

s_order is the order $k = p+1$, the same convention as SimpleSplines.order — so s_order = 4 is the cubic basis both collision-operator manuscripts specify, and s_order = 3 is quadratic.

length_big_cell > 0 appends one oversized cell of that width at each end of domain, outside it, giving a GeneralMesh. That is the device for keeping a particle that strays out of the resolved region inside the support of the basis; with 0 the mesh is uniform on domain exactly.

domain is anything with a first and a last — a two-tuple, a vector or a range.

bc may be a SimpleSplines.BoundaryCondition, a two-tuple for the two ends, or one of the symbols :Dirichlet, :Periodic and :nothing that the drivers in scripts/ use, where :nothing means Free(). Any other symbol is an error. The default is Free() — the unconstrained clamped basis, which is the only choice that reproduces $1$, $v$ and $v^2$ and hence the only one on which the conservative schemes conserve.

The mass matrix is always the exact Galerkin one, $\mathbb{M}_{ij} = \int \varphi_i \varphi_j$; there is no lumped-mass option.

VlasovMethods.SplineDistribution — Type
SplineDistribution{DT, XD, VD}

The distribution function represented as a spline in velocity, $f_s(v) = \sum_I \hat{f}_I \, \varphi_I(v)$, together with everything needed to project onto it and to integrate against it.

VD is the number of velocity dimensions. For VD == 1 the basis is a one-dimensional SimpleSplines B-spline basis and the coefficients are a vector; for VD > 1 it is a TensorProductBasis and the coefficients are a VD-dimensional array. Nothing else about the two cases differs — the evaluation, the projection and the mass solve are the same calls.

Fields

  • spline — the SimpleSplines.Spline, which shares coefficients. A projection writes that array, and every reference to the spline sees the new function without a rebuild.
  • basis — the spline basis.
  • quadrature — the assembly table: the basis tabulated at the Gauß-Legendre points, the weights, and the mass operator.
  • coefficients — the degrees of freedom $\hat{f}$.

The mass matrix is never formed as a Kronecker product

For VD > 1 the mass matrix is $\mathbb{M}^{(VD)} \otimes \dots \otimes \mathbb{M}^{(1)}$, and a solve is VD one-dimensional solves applied along each axis. That is an identity, not an approximation, and it is what makes the higher-dimensional cases tractable at all: forming the product and factorising it densely would mean a 1681×1681 matrix for the 41-element cubic basis of a two-dimensional velocity space, and 68921² in three dimensions, which does not fit.

The boundary condition is not a free choice

The $L^2$ projection reproduces $\int \pi(v) f \, dv$ for a polynomial $\pi$ exactly when $\pi$ lies in the span of the basis, so the mass, momentum and energy of $f_s$ agree with those of the particles only if $1$, $v$ and $v^2$ are in the span. polynomial_reproduction(basis) reports the largest degree available: p for the clamped basis (Free()), 0 for a periodic one — $v$ is not periodic — and -1 for a Dirichlet-recombined one, where not even the constants survive.

Measured on a cubic basis over $[-8,8]$ by scripts/verify_conservation.jl: Free() reproduces all three to $10^{-14}$, Periodic() gets $v$ wrong by $1.2$ and $v^2$ by $0.12$, and Dirichlet() gets even the constant wrong by $4 \times 10^{-2}$.

What this does and does not break

It does not by itself break the particle-level conservation of the conservative Lenard-Bernstein operator: its coefficients $A_1, A_2$ are solved from the two conservation constraints, which are conditions on particle sums, so $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha \dot{v}_\alpha$ and $\sum_\alpha w_\alpha v_\alpha \dot{v}_\alpha$ vanish at round-off on any basis. What it breaks is the agreement between the particle and spline representations, and therefore every diagnostic computed from $f_s$ — the entropy above all, which is what the H-theorem is a statement about. See check_conservation_basis.

VlasovMethods.check_conservation_basis — Method
check_conservation_basis(dist::SplineDistribution; moments = 2)

Throw unless the basis reproduces every polynomial of degree ≤ moments, i.e. unless the moments $\int v^k f_s \, dv$ for k ≤ moments agree with the particles'.

Not called automatically: a diagnostic run may legitimately want a Dirichlet or periodic basis, and refusing to build one would be wrong. Call it where a result depends on the two representations agreeing — before reporting an entropy, or before comparing a spline moment against a particle one.

VlasovMethods.GridDistribution — Type
GridDistribution{DT, XT, VT, FT}

The distribution function of a 1D1V phase space, stored as its values at the nodes of an nx × nv grid. It is a DistributionFunction{DT, 1, 1}, beside ParticleDistribution and SplineDistribution.

The grid

  • x is periodic: $x_i = x_a + (i-1) h_x$ for $i = 1, \dots, n_x$, with $h_x = (x_b - x_a) / n_x$. The node $x_b$ is the node $x_a$ again and is not stored.
  • v is bounded: $v_j = v_a + (j-1) h_v$ for $j = 1, \dots, n_v$, with $h_v = (v_b - v_a) / (n_v - 1)$. Both ends of the range are nodes.

values is the nx × nv matrix $f_{ij} = f(x_i, v_j)$. Its column-major linear order, x running fastest, is the order the grid stencils such as _apply_∫dv! use, so vec(dist.values) is their state vector.

Evaluation

dist(x, v) interpolates bilinearly between the four surrounding nodes. It is periodic in x and zero outside $[v_a, v_b]$.

Moments

velocity_moments integrates over v by the rectangle rule, $\int f(x_i, v) \, dv \approx \sum_j f_{ij} h_v$. This is the integral of the interpolant plus $\tfrac{h_v}{2} (f_{i1} + f_{i n_v})$, so it is exact up to the values at the two ends of the velocity range and first order in $h_v$ where those do not vanish.

VlasovMethods.velocity_moments — Method
velocity_moments(dist::GridDistribution)

The velocity moments of dist at each x-node, as the named tuple (density, momentum, energy) of three vectors of length nx:

\[n_i = \sum_j f_{ij} h_v , \qquad p_i = \sum_j v_j f_{ij} h_v , \qquad \varepsilon_i = \frac{1}{2} \sum_j v_j^2 f_{ij} h_v .\]

energy is the kinetic energy density, with the factor $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The sums are the rectangle rule of _apply_∫dv! and its moment stencils.

VlasovMethods.MaxwellianDistribution — Method
MaxwellianDistribution(v)

The normalised Maxwellian of unit temperature and zero mean in d = length(v) velocity dimensions,

\[f_M(v) = (2\pi)^{-d/2} \, \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2} |v|^2\right) , \qquad \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} f_M \, dv = 1 .\]

The normalisation is dimension-dependent

It was fixed at $1/(2\pi)$, which is the $d = 2$ value. That was correct where the function was used — project_Maxwellian existed only for a two-dimensional velocity space — but it is wrong for any other d, and silently so: in one dimension it is short by a factor $\sqrt{2\pi}$, so a "normalised" Maxwellian integrated to $0.399$ rather than to $1$. The Lenard-Bernstein manuscript's $f_M(v) = e^{-v^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ is the $d = 1$ case of the expression above.

VlasovMethods.CollisionEntropy — Method
(ent::CollisionEntropy)()

The discrete entropy

\[S_h = \int_\Omega f_s \, \log |f_s| \, dv = \frac{1}{2} \int_\Omega f_s \, \log (f_s^2) \, dv ,\]

eq:disc_entropy of the Landau and Lenard-Bernstein metriplectic manuscripts, evaluated on the Gauß-Legendre grid the spline basis already carries, in any number of velocity dimensions.

$S_h$ is the H-functional of the manuscripts' $S = \int f \log f$: it decreases under the collision operators. $\log |f_s|$ equals $\log (f_s)$ where $f_s > 0$, and stays defined where the $L^2$ projection of a particle distribution undershoots below zero. A node where $f_s$ is zero or not finite throws a DomainError, because $\log |f_s|$ is undefined there.

initialize!(dist::ParticleDistribution, example::VlasovExample[, sampling])

Every example writes the velocities and the weights of the particles. Some write the positions as well, and some leave them as they are; the docstring of each example says which. sampling selects the sampling method of the examples that sample by rejection: BumpOnTail takes AcceptRejectSampling(), its default, or ImportanceSampling(), and Bump takes AcceptRejectSampling() only. VlasovMethods.NormalDistribution — Type
NormalDistribution(domain = (0.0, 1.0))

Normal velocities on positions in domain.

initialize! draws N standard normal values of the first position component, shifts and scales them into [0, 1] by the integer ceil(maximum(abs, x)), and maps that interval onto domain. It then draws every velocity component from the standard normal distribution. Each particle has the weight 1/N. The other position components are not written.

The draws use the global random number generator, so a caller seeds it with Random.seed! to repeat a sample. The positions and the velocities are independent draws.

VlasovMethods.Bump — Type
Bump(; a = 10.0, b = 0.5)

The smooth top hat of the half width b and the steepness a in the velocity,

\[f(v) = \frac{1}{4b} \bigl( \tanh(a (v + b)) - \tanh(a (v - b)) \bigr) ,\]

which integrates to one. initialize! fills the velocities of a 1D1V distribution by accept–reject sampling of proposals in $[-b, b]$, as draw! describes, and does not write the positions. The sample therefore follows $f$ conditioned on $[-b, b]$, without the tails of $f$ beyond $\pm b$.

VlasovMethods.BumpOnTail — Type
BumpOnTail(; ε = 0.03, κ = 0.3, α = 0.1, σ = 0.5, v₀ = 4.5)

The bump-on-tail distribution of the reduced-order experiments,

\[f(x, v) = \bigl(1 - \varepsilon \cos(\kappa x)\bigr) \, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left( (1 - \alpha) \, e^{-v^2/2} + \frac{\alpha}{\sigma} \, e^{-(v - v_0)^2 / (2\sigma^2)} \right) ,\]

on the periodic interval $[0, 2\pi/\kappa)$: a Maxwellian bulk and a fraction α of particles in a beam of the mean velocity v₀ and the width σ, with a perturbation of the amplitude ε and the wave number κ in the density.

initialize! fills a 1D1V distribution, by AcceptRejectSampling(), the default, or by ImportanceSampling(); draw! describes both.

VlasovMethods.draw! — Method
draw!(dist, fₓ, params::BumpOnTail, ::AcceptRejectSampling)

Fill the 1D1V distribution dist with the bump-on-tail particles of params, and return it. Each position is a Sobol proposal, uniform on [0, 2π/κ], accepted with the probability fₓ(x)/(1 + ε), and each particle has the weight 2π/κ/N. VlasovMethods.AnisotropicBiMaxwellian — Type
AnisotropicBiMaxwellian(σ1², σ2²; seed = 20261107)

The centred bi-Maxwellian of the two temperatures σ1² and σ2²,

\[f(v) = \frac{1}{2 \pi \sigma_1 \sigma_2} \exp\left(-\frac{v_1^2}{2 \sigma_1^2} - \frac{v_2^2}{2 \sigma_2^2}\right) ,\]

the 2D reference case whose second moments isotropise: at the Maxwell molecules of γ = 0, with ν = 1 and weights that sum to one, the difference T₁₁ − T₂₂ of its second moments decays at the rate 8, which scripts/reference_forms.jl derives from the moment equations.

initialize! fills the two velocity components of a ParticleDistribution with a sample of f drawn from Random.MersenneTwister(seed), one stream of N normal variates per component, so that the same seed gives the same sample within one Julia version. The positions are not touched, and the weights are set to 1/N. The sample's means, variances and covariance approach the exact 0, 0, σ1², σ2² and 0 at the standard errors σ/√N, σ²√(2/N) and σ₁σ₂/√N of its N particles. A variance that is not positive and finite throws an ArgumentError, and an initialize! of a distribution whose velocities are not two components a DimensionMismatch: neither is a bi-Maxwellian this package can sample.

VlasovMethods.DoubleMaxwellian2D — Type
DoubleMaxwellian2D(; weights = (0.5, 0.5), variances = ((1.0, 1.0), (1.0, 1.0)),
                   means = ((0.0, 0.0), (0.0, 0.0)), seed = 20261107)

The sum of two displaced centred Maxwellians of the weights weights, the variances variances and the means means,

\[f(v) = w_1 \, g_1(v - m_1) + w_2 \, g_2(v - m_2) ,\]

with g_c the Gaussian of the diagonal covariance variances[c] and the mean means[c], each a pair of two velocity components: the 2D reference case whose second moments relax to those of the mixture. Its exact mean is w_1 m_1 + w_2 m_2 and its exact variances are Σ_c w_c (σ²_c + m²_c) less the square of that mean.

initialize! gives the first component the share round(weights[1] * N) of the N particles, draws each from Random.MersenneTwister(seed) and gives the rest to the second, so that the realised weights are those counts over N and the same seed gives the same sample within one Julia version. The positions are not touched, and the weights are set to 1/N. The constructor refuses what a mixture of two components cannot be, each with an ArgumentError: other than two weights, variances or means, a variance or mean pair of other than two velocity components, a weight that is negative or that does not sum with its twin to one, a variance that is not positive and finite, and a mean that is not finite.

Example

dist = initialize!(ParticleDistribution(1, 2, 10_000),
                   DoubleMaxwellian2D(weights = (0.6, 0.4),
                                      variances = ((0.5, 0.3), (0.3, 0.5)),
                                      means = ((0.4, 0.0), (-0.4, 0.0))))

VlasovMethods.BKWDistribution — Type
BKWDistribution(t₀; seed = 20261107)

The two-dimensional BKW solution of the homogeneous Landau equation at the time t₀, the third 2D reference case: at the Maxwell molecules of γ = 0 and ν = 1 it is an exact solution, and its departure from the Maxwellian decays at a rate that scripts/reference_forms.jl derives from the moment equations.

At d = 2 the profile of Carrillo, Hu, Wang and Wu (2020), Appendix A, is

\[f(v) = \frac{1}{2 \pi K} \exp\left(-\frac{v^2}{2K}\right) \left[ \frac{4K - 2}{2K} + \frac{1 - K}{2K^2} \, v^2 \right] , \qquad K(t) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} e^{-2t} ,\]

of mass one and temperature one, whose second moment is the identity at every t. The paper states K(t) = 1 - e^{-t/8}/2 for its kernel A(z) = (|z|² I - z ⊗ z)/16; this package's kernel U(z) = |z|² (I - ẑ ẑᵀ) is 16 A, and the sixteenfold kernel multiplies every rate by sixteen.

initialize! fills the two velocity components of a ParticleDistribution with a sample of the profile drawn from Random.MersenneTwister(seed). The profile is a Gaussian of covariance K I of the fraction (2K - 1)/K, and that Gaussian weighted by |v|² for the rest, whose squared radius is 2K times a Gamma(2, 1) variate: a sum of two standard exponentials. The sample's means are 0 and its variances 1, within the standard errors 1/√N and √(2/N) that a Gaussian sample of its N particles has: the profile's own error of a variance is the smaller √((3K(2 - K) - 1)/N). Its fourth moment is that of the profile rather than the Gaussian's 8. The positions are not touched, and the weights are set to 1/N. A non-finite t₀ throws an ArgumentError, and a negative one too: the quadratic factor of the profile is negative near v = 0 there, and the profile is no distribution.

VlasovMethods.bkw_parameter — Method
bkw_parameter(t) -> Float64

The parameter K(t) = 1 - exp(-2t)/2 of the BKW profile at the time t, at the kernel U(z) = |z|² (I - ẑ ẑᵀ) of γ = 0 and ν = 1: the paper's K(t) = 1 - exp(-t/8)/2 at a rate sixteen times its own, since U = 16 A for its kernel A. K(0) = 1/2 is the profile that vanishes quadratically at v = 0, and K(t) → 1 is the Maxwellian.

VlasovMethods.projection! — Method
projection!(potential::PoissonSolvers.Potential, distribution::ParticleDistribution)

Deposit the particle weights of distribution onto the basis of potential, writing the charge density into the potential's right-hand side buffer.

Each particle contributes to the local_width(basis) basis functions that do not vanish at its position. evaluate_all! reduces a periodic argument into the domain and writes those values into a buffer, returning the index of the first; basis_index is what wraps it, and is the identity where the basis is not periodic. The particle state keeps the unwrapped position, so the reduction belongs to the evaluation.

A recombined basis has no single width: a function near an end spans the union of two parent supports, so local_width is the largest block and a cell with fewer nonzero functions leaves the tail of the buffer zero. Those padding entries carry indices past nbasis(basis), which is why the zeros are skipped rather than added.

project_Maxwellian(sdist::SplineDistribution)

Project the normalised Maxwellian onto the basis of sdist.

The projection goes through the Gauß-Legendre quadrature the basis already carries, in any number of velocity dimensions. project_function calls f with a scalar when VD == 1 and with a VD-tuple otherwise, and SVector accepts both, so one method covers every dimension.

VlasovMethods.project_function — Method
project_function(f, sdist::SplineDistribution)

The $L^2$ projection of the function f onto the basis of sdist, written into its coefficients.

f is called with a scalar for a one-dimensional velocity space and with a VD-tuple otherwise. This is eq:numerical-example-1-coefficients of the Lenard-Bernstein manuscript, $\hat{f} = \mathbb{M}^{-1} \int f \varphi_i \, dv$, and it works in any number of velocity dimensions rather than only in two.

source
VlasovMethods.projection — Method
projection(velocities, dist::ParticleDistribution, final_dist::SplineDistribution)

Project the particles onto the basis of final_dist, writing the coefficients into it and returning its Spline.

velocities is a vector for a one-dimensional velocity space and a VD × N matrix otherwise; it is passed separately from dist because the implicit solvers evaluate the right-hand side at a stage value rather than at the particles' stored positions.

A particle outside the domain is an error, not a warning

The velocity domain is truncated and the true one is not, so a particle can leave. When it does there is nothing sensible to do with it: it contributes nothing to the projection, so its weight is lost from $f_s$ while it keeps moving, and f_s then evaluates to zero at its position — which makes the $f_s'/f_s$ of every collision operator here a division by zero. The Lenard-Bernstein manuscript says as much in its closing remarks.

A DomainError naming the count and the domain is therefore the only useful response: a run that has lost mass is not a run whose output means anything. Enlarge the domain, or give the mesh oversized end cells with length_big_cell.
