Grid-based reduced tensors

VlasovMethods.PotentialReducedTensor — Type
PotentialReducedTensor(tensor::GridTensor{DT, 3}, Pi, Pj, Pk)

The lazy size(Pi, 2) × size(Pj, 2) × size(Pk, 2) array that projects the first two indices of the bracket tensor tensor onto the columns of Pi and Pj, and its third index onto the columns of Pk. With R = ReducedTensor(tensor, Pi, Pj),

PotentialReducedTensor[i, j, k] = ∑_K R[i, j, K] * Pk[K, k] .

Pi, Pj and Pk are matrices with one row per grid node. The nodes are counted in the order of the grid's own linear indices, the first direction fastest, as in vec(dist.values) of a GridDistribution. The grid may have any dimension.

The constructor builds ReducedTensor(tensor, Pi, Pj) first, so the checks of that constructor run here as well: Pi and Pj need one row per node, or it throws a DimensionMismatch, their element type has to be DT, or it throws an ArgumentError, and every direction of the grid needs at least 2w + 1 nodes for the tensor's stencil width w, or it throws an ArgumentError. Pk needs one row per node, or the constructor throws a DimensionMismatch, and the element type DT, or it throws an ArgumentError. An index off the array throws a BoundsError.

The tensor's contract is ReducedTensor's and is assumed, not checked: tensor is a bracket's tensor on the grid that bracket was built on, nonzero only where its stencil says so. bracket_tensor builds such a tensor from an Arakawa. A tensor written by hand on another grid than its bracket breaks the contract silently.

getindex allocates nothing, and neither does materialising the array into a preallocated output.

julia> using GeometricBrackets: Arakawa

julia> gt = bracket_tensor(Arakawa(3, 3, 0.5, 0.5));

julia> size(PotentialReducedTensor(gt, ones(9, 2), ones(9, 1), ones(9, 4)))
(2, 1, 4)

VlasovMethods.FullyReducedTensor — Method
FullyReducedTensor(tensor::GridTensor{DT, 3}, Pi, Pj, Px)

The lazy size(Pi, 2) × size(Pj, 2) × size(Px, 2) array that projects the third index of the bracket tensor tensor onto the columns of Px, read at the x index of the node. With R = ReducedTensor(tensor, Pi, Pj) and (n₁, n₂) the grid indices of the node K,

FullyReducedTensor[i, j, k] = ∑_K R[i, j, K] * Px[n₁, k] .

Px has one row per x node, in the order of dist.x, and not one per grid node. The result is the PotentialReducedTensor whose projection has the row Px[n₁, :] for each node.

The grid needs two dimensions, or the function throws an ArgumentError. Px needs nx rows, or it throws a DimensionMismatch, and the element type DT, or it throws an ArgumentError. PotentialReducedTensor gives the other checks, the node order, the tensor's contract and the allocations.

julia> size(FullyReducedTensor(gt, ones(9, 2), ones(9, 1), ones(3, 4)))   # gt as above
(2, 1, 4)

VlasovMethods.VelocityReducedMatrix — Method
VelocityReducedMatrix(tensor::GridTensor{DT, 3}, Pi, Pj, v²)

The lazy size(Pi, 2) × size(Pj, 2) matrix that contracts the third index of the bracket tensor tensor with v²/2. With R = ReducedTensor(tensor, Pi, Pj) and (n₁, n₂) the grid indices of the node K,

VelocityReducedMatrix[i, j] = ∑_K R[i, j, K] * v²[n₂] / 2 .

v² holds the squared velocity nodes, dist.v .^ 2 of a GridDistribution: one entry per velocity node, in the order of dist.v. The result is view(prt, :, :, 1) of the PotentialReducedTensor prt whose one-column projection carries v²[n₂] / 2 in the row of each node.

The grid needs two dimensions, or the function throws an ArgumentError. v² needs nv entries, or it throws a DimensionMismatch, and the element type DT, or it throws an ArgumentError. PotentialReducedTensor gives the other checks, the node order, the tensor's contract and the allocations.

julia> size(VelocityReducedMatrix(gt, ones(9, 2), ones(9, 1), [1.0, 4.0, 9.0]))   # gt as above
(2, 1)

VlasovMethods.bracket_tensor — Method
bracket_tensor(a::Arakawa{DT})

The rank-3 GridTensor of the bracket a on a's own nx × nv grid, with stencil width 1: its entry at the nodes (I, J, K) is a(I, J, K). The grid is read from a, so the tensor cannot be on another grid than its bracket. This is the tensor that PotentialReducedTensor, VelocityReducedMatrix and FullyReducedTensor take.

GeometricBrackets' four-argument PoissonTensor(DT, nx, nv, f) builds the same tensor and checks the grid, but it returns a bracket that wraps the tensor, and these three take the GridTensor itself.

julia> using GeometricBrackets: Arakawa

julia> size(bracket_tensor(Arakawa(3, 4, 0.5, 0.5)))
(12, 12, 12)

VlasovMethods._get_MC̃_cubic — Method
_get_MC̃_cubic(V, ∫dv, ∫vdv, ∫v²dv, v, ci, li, h₁, h₂)

Assemble the cubic, four-index collision tensor MC̃[o, a, b, c] on the n₁ × n₂ phase-space grid, from the evaluation matrix V, the three moment matrices ∫dv, ∫vdv and ∫v²dv of the rectangle rule, the velocity grid v, the CartesianIndices ci and the LinearIndices li of the n₁ × n₂ grid, and the grid widths h₁ and h₂.

v holds one entry per velocity node, so the call throws a DimensionMismatch under length(v) ≠ size(ci, 2). The tensor has the element type eltype(V).

Contracted with the grid values, Q₃[o] = Σ MC̃[o, a, b, c] f[a] f[b] f[c] is the Galerkin form of ρ²·C[f]. It conserves the discrete moments ∫dv·f, ∫vdv·f and ∫v²dv·f — the mass, the momentum and the energy — while f vanishes at the ends of the v-grid to eps(T). The v-stencil wraps the bounded grid with mod1, so a non-negligible end value breaks the conservation.

VlasovMethods._get_MC̃_quadratic — Method
_get_MC̃_quadratic(V, ∫dv, ∫vdv, ∫v²dv, v, ci, li, h₁, h₂)

Assemble the quadratic, three-index collision tensor MC̃[o, a, c] on the n₁ × n₂ phase-space grid, from the same arguments as _get_MC̃_cubic. v holds one entry per velocity node, so the call throws a DimensionMismatch under length(v) ≠ size(ci, 2), and the tensor has the element type eltype(V).

Contracted with the grid values, Q₂[o] = Σ MC̃[o, a, c] f[a] f[c] is the Galerkin form of ρ²·C[f] without the ρ̂u term. It conserves the mass ∫dv·f and the energy ∫v²dv·f, but not the momentum ∫vdv·f: it pulls the mean velocity toward v = 0, by a relative 1.3e-2 over ten RK4 steps of Δt = 10⁻³ on nx = 2, v = range(-10, 10; length = 25), from the shifted Maxwellian of the invariants testset in test/gridbased/collisions.jl. The moments are conserved only while f vanishes at the ends of the v-grid to eps(T).

VlasovMethods.ElectricField — Type
ElectricField

The electric field of a one-dimensional Vlasov–Poisson problem on particles.

A field deposits particle positions with their weights, solves for the potential, and evaluates the field $E(x) = -\partial_x \phi(x)$ at particle positions. Its interface is two functions and three call forms:

update!(f, x, w, t)     # deposit the positions `x` with the weights `w` at the time `t`
efield!(f, e, x)        # write `E` at each position of `x` into `e`
f(e, x, w, t)           # `update!` followed by `efield!`
f(e, x)                 # `efield!` alone
f(x, w, t)              # both, into an `e` that call allocates

update! and efield! are called once per time step of a march, so a concrete field holds the buffers it needs and neither function allocates. energy(f) and coefficients(f) read the field energy and the potential coefficients of f. A reduced field, such as ReducedElectricField, holds a full-order field and lifts into it.

update! and efield! are not exported, so a caller reaches them through the three call forms above, as VlasovMethods.update! and VlasovMethods.efield!, or with using VlasovMethods: update!, efield!. Neither is the PoissonSolvers.update! that a PoissonField calls on its own potential: that one solves the potential's linear system, and it is a different function that happens to carry the same name.

VlasovMethods.PoissonField — Type
PoissonField(potential)

The full-order electric field of the PoissonSolvers Potential potential, the field a particle VlasovPoisson model integrates.

The deposit of update! is _deposit!, the field of efield! is _electric_field, and the solve is the potential's own PoissonSolvers.update!.

potential is on a B-spline basis, as the potential of a particle model is. The constructor holds the buffer of the local_width(basis) basis values that the deposit and the field evaluation fill, and the quadrature of the basis that energy integrates $(\partial_x\phi)^2$ with, so that neither allocates on a call.

VlasovMethods.ScaledField — Type
ScaledField(field::ElectricField, χ::Real)

The full-order field as the rescaled state of a march sees it: efield! divides the field of field by χ², and energy divides its energy by the same χ². That is not the energy of the divided field, which is quadratic in the field and would carry χ⁴. update! and coefficients forward to field unchanged, because the deposit and the solve carry no amplitude.

It is an ElectricField, so a reduced march wraps it as ReducedElectricField(ScaledField(field, χ), Ψ, X). A march takes its scale from params.χ, so it marches a ScaledField, or a reduced field over one, only when the χ stored here equals params.χ, and throws an ArgumentError that names both values otherwise. It refuses a ScaledField over a ScaledField at any χ (see integrate_vp).

χ must be finite and positive; the constructor throws an ArgumentError otherwise, which refuses 0, a negative and NaN alike. Each division converts χ to the element type of the value it divides, so a Float32 field with a Float64 χ keeps Float32.

VlasovMethods.ReducedElectricField — Type
ReducedElectricField(field, Ψₓ, Ψₚ, Ψₑ, X)
ReducedElectricField(field, Ψ, X)

The reduced electric field of the full-order field: the lift, solve and project path, with no hyper-reduction. The reduced coordinates X are lifted to the particle positions by Ψₓ, the field is deposited from them and solved, evaluated at the positions lifted by Ψₚ, and projected back by Ψₑ:

update!(f, X, w, t)     # deposit  Ψₓ X  and solve
efield!(f, e, X)        # e = Ψₑ E(Ψₚ X)

A DEIMElectricField of a field basis Ψₑ with DEIM row indices p is the same path, lifted by Ψₓ[p, :] and projected by ΨₓᵀΨₑ Ψₑ[p, :]⁻¹, which evaluates the field at the kₑ interpolation points p instead of at every particle.

For an orthonormal basis Ψ, the three-argument form is the full lift and the transpose projection, ReducedElectricField(field, Ψ, X) = ReducedElectricField(field, Ψ, Ψ, Ψ', X). At full basis size, the reduced field equals the projection of the full-order field onto the basis. See ElectricField for the interface, and DEIMElectricField for the hyper-reduced path.

VlasovMethods.DEIMElectricField — Method
DEIMElectricField(field, Ψₓ, Ψₑ, p, X)
DEIMElectricField(field, Ψₓ, Ψₑ, Πₑ, X)

The hyper-reduced electric field of the full-order field: a ReducedElectricField lifted by Ψₓ[p, :] and projected by ΨₓᵀΨₑ Ψₑ[p, :]⁻¹, where p are the DEIM row indices of the field basis Ψₑ, from ReducedBasisMethods.get_DEIM_indices. The second form takes the DEIM interpolation matrix Πₑ of get_DEIM_interpolation_matrix, reads the row of the nonzero entry of each column, and calls the first; it does not check that Πₑ is a selection matrix. The buffers of the field follow the element type of Ψₓ and X, and X is the reduced coordinate they are sized from. With Πₑ = I[:, p], the lift of the field evaluation is

\[E_{\text{DEIM}} = \Psi_x^T \Psi_e (\Pi_e^T \Psi_e)^{-1} E(\Pi_e^T \Psi_x X),\]

so the field is evaluated at the $k_e$ interpolation points rather than at every particle. For orthonormal position and field bases, the reduced acceleration agrees with the non-hyper-reduced one to the DEIM error bound of the paper ReducedBasisMethods.get_DEIM_interpolation_matrix cites.

VlasovMethods.Snapshots — Type
Snapshots{T, PS}

The snapshots of a parameter sweep: the positions X, the velocities V, the accelerations A, the potentials Φ, and the diagnostics W, K and M, on the sweep's ParameterSpace.

X, V and A are indexed [component, particle, time, sample] and have the size (nd, nₚ, nₛ, nparam), with nparam = length(paramspace). X[:, :, t, p] is the (nd, nₚ) matrix of the positions saved at the t-th of the nₛ saved times of the sample paramspace(p), X[i, j, t, p] is component i of particle j, and V and A hold the velocities and the accelerations of that state the same way. So vec(X[:, :, t, p]) is the component-fastest order of vec(particles.x) of that state, and a snapshot matrix built from these arrays has one column per saved time and sample.

Φ has the size (nh, nₛ, nparam): the potential is a scalar on the one dimension of the Poisson problem, so it carries no component axis, and nh is the number of degrees of freedom of the sweep's Poisson solver. W, K and M have the size (nₛ, nparam), one value each per saved time and sample. W is the field energy, not a particle weight: the snapshots store no weights.

The component count nd of X, V and A and the degree count nh of Φ are the leading extents of those arrays, so they are read from the arrays rather than stored beside them.

== compares all eight fields, paramspace included.

VlasovMethods.IntegratorParameters — Type
IntegratorParameters(dt, nₜ, nₛ, nₕ, nₚ, nparam)

The parameters of a reduced sweep: the timestep dt, the number of time steps nₜ, the number of saved times nₛ — the initial state included — the number of potential degrees of freedom nₕ, the number of particles nₚ and the number of sampled equation parameters nparam. t holds nₛ times, evenly spaced from 0 to dt·nₜ.

The constructor throws an ArgumentError when nₜ < 1, when nₛ < 2, and when nₛ - 1 does not divide nₜ, so that the saves of the march would write past nₛ or drop the final state. The HDF5 constructor applies the same checks to the stored counts.

Every count is Int, so an Int32 count is a MethodError. The element type T of dt is shared by P₀, Ψ, SS and IC in reduced_integrate_vp, so mixed storage types are a MethodError. The timestep scale params.χ is converted to T by the march.

The HDF5 pair is h5save(h5, IP; path = …) and IntegratorParameters(h5, path). h5save writes into the group at path, and creates it when it does not exist.

VlasovMethods.integrate_vp — Method
integrate_vp(P₀, params, efield, SS, IP, p; save = true)

The full-order leapfrog march of an ElectricField, the same march as reduced_integrate_vp without a basis: the state of P₀ advanced IP.nₜ steps of IP.dt by full_leapfrog_step!, with one kick of the full field of efield per step. A reduced model marches the ScaledField of the same field, so the two marches of one comparison take the same field and the same χ, which is params.χ. With nsave = nₜ/(nₛ - 1), the state of every nsave-th step it, the initial state included, is written into the save div(it, nsave) + 1 of the sample p of the snapshots SS by save_full_solution.

The march holds its own buffers, so efield is the only state it shares with the caller: P₀ is copied and never written, and the march needs no cache argument.

params is the caller's, and it must hold χ. The χ scale is a timestep scale and nothing else: the step is Δt = IP.dt·T(params.χ), converted to the march's element type before the product, and the times handed to the field are unscaled: the kick of step it is at its midpoint (it - ½)·IP.dt, and the save of step it at it·IP.dt. So a time-independent field marched with (dt, χ = 2) equals the same field marched with (2dt, χ = 1) state for state.

params.χ is the one source of the scale. When efield is a ScaledField, or a ReducedElectricField over one, the χ the ScaledField was built with must equal params.χ, and the field and its energy are divided by the χ² of the step. Any other field is marched as it is.

It returns nothing; the states are in SS, and the integration parameters hold the times.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when the snapshots carry another number of components than one; when params.χ is not finite and > 0, which refuses 0, a negative, Inf and NaN alike, as ScaledField does; when efield is a ScaledField, or a ReducedElectricField over one, built with another χ than params.χ, naming both; and when that ScaledField is over a ScaledField, at any χ. The counts of IP are checked by IntegratorParameters itself.
  • DimensionMismatch when the snapshots do not hold IP.nₛ saved times, and when the particle list holds another number of particles than the snapshots do.
  • The NamedTuple's own error when params holds no χ. There is no default.

Cost

The loop's two calls — full_leapfrog_step! and save_full_solution — are inferred and allocate nothing. The buffers of the state are allocated once, before the loop.

VlasovMethods.reduced_integrate_vp — Method
reduced_integrate_vp(P₀, Ψ, params, efield, SS, IP, IC, p; save = true)

The reduced leapfrog march of the analytic quadratic model: the reduced state of P₀ — the two coordinates Ψ'·vec(P₀.x) and Ψ'·vec(P₀.v) — advanced IP.nₜ steps of IP.dt by leapfrog_step!, with one kick of the reduced acceleration of efield per step. With nsave = nₜ/(nₛ - 1), the state of every nsave-th step it, the initial state included, is written into the save div(it, nsave) + 1 of the sample p of the snapshots SS by save_solution. Ψ is the position basis, and the same basis lifts the velocities: the cotangent lift's Φ.

params is the caller's, and it must hold χ. The χ scale is a timestep scale and nothing else: the step is Δt = IP.dt·T(params.χ), converted to the march's element type before the product, and the times handed to the field are unscaled: the kick of step it is at its midpoint (it - ½)·IP.dt, and the save of step it at it·IP.dt. So a time-independent field marched with (dt, χ = 2) equals the same field marched with (2dt, χ = 1) state for state.

params.χ is the one source of the scale. When efield is a ReducedElectricField over a ScaledField, the χ the ScaledField was built with must equal params.χ, and the march takes the field as it is. Any other field is marched as it is.

It returns nothing; the states are in SS, and the integration parameters hold the times.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when the snapshots carry another number of components than one; when the basis has a zero column count or more columns than rows; when params.χ is not > 0, which refuses 0, a negative and NaN alike; when efield is over a ScaledField built with another χ than params.χ, naming both; and when efield is over a ScaledField over a ScaledField, at any χ. The counts of IP are checked by IntegratorParameters itself.
  • DimensionMismatch when the snapshots do not hold IP.nₛ saved times.
  • The NamedTuple's own error when params holds no χ. There is no default.

Cost

The loop's two calls — leapfrog_step! and save_solution — are inferred and allocate nothing.

energy_drift(H)

The relative drift of an energy history H: (H .- H[1]) ./ H[1], the energy at each saved time against the initial one.

The energies of a conservative model are constant, so the drift is its discretisation error and H[1] is its reference. NaN and Inf in H propagate into the result.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when H is empty, and when H[1] is zero, because a relative drift needs a non-zero reference.

VlasovMethods.field_energy_error — Method
field_energy_error(Wh, Wr)

The relative error of a reduced field energy against a full-order one, abs.(Wh - Wr) ./ Wh, per saved time and parameter.

A field energy is ½ϕᵀKϕ ≥ 0 by construction, so the division is by a non-negative number and no guard is needed: a Wh of 0 gives Inf, or NaN where Wr is also 0, and both propagate.

Wh and Wr must have the same size; a difference raises a DimensionMismatch naming both.

VlasovMethods.growth_rate — Method
growth_rate(t, W; window = 2, maxima = 2:5)

The growth rate $γ$ of the field energy history W at the times t: half the least-squares slope of $\log W$ over the selected local maxima, because $W ∝ |E|² ∝ e^{2γt}$. The times are physical time, so this is the physical growth rate.

A maximum is a point no lower than the window points on each side, whose first differing sample on its right is lower. A flat maximum counts once, at its first index, and a rising shoulder, a plateau that ends in a higher sample, is no maximum. maxima selects which of them the fit uses, by their order along t, which drops the first few maxima; the fit runs in eltype(W), so it takes no dependency on Statistics.

A matrix W gives one γ per column, as a Vector of the element type of W.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when window < 1; when maxima names an index twice, naming it, because one maximum may not enter the fit twice; when it selects fewer than two maxima, or reaches past the maxima found, each naming the number found; when it selects an index below 1; and when W is not positive at a selected maximum, where $\log W$ is not defined.
  • DimensionMismatch when t and W differ in length, naming both.

VlasovMethods.AnalyticHamiltonian — Type
AnalyticHamiltonian(β)

The Hamiltonian of the analytic quadratic-potential model, $H(q, p) = ½|p|² + ½β²|q|²$, for a state x = [q; p] of 2N entries, and the ∇H! of the reduced forms of ReducedBasisMethods. The two halves are the two halves of x.

The instance is both:

  • the gradient, called as ∇H!(g, t, x, params), which writes ∇H(x) — the first block β²q and the second p — into g and returns it. It is what ReducedBasisMethods.reduced_ode and ReducedBasisMethods.reduced_pode take as their ∇H!, and it is not RBM's hamiltonian_gradient!, which is the reduced gradient of a SymplecticDEIM;
  • the Hamiltonian, called as h(t, x, params), which returns H(x).

Neither call reads t or params: the model is autonomous and takes no equation parameters. The gradient accepts any element type, which Newton's JacobianAutodiff needs — the Lobatto IIIA–IIIB(2) run of the PODE form calls the vector field with ForwardDiff.Dual states.

Example

h = AnalyticHamiltonian(6.0)
z₀ = project(b, x₀)
prob = reduced_pode(b, h, (0.0, 1000.0), 1e-4, z₀)

VlasovMethods.train_hnn — Function
train_hnn(model, ps₀, data, target; η, epochs, batchsize, rng, batches = nothing)
    -> (ps, losses)

Train the Hamiltonian neural network model on the reduced field of a bump-on-tail sweep, and return its parameters and the loss of every epoch.

Defined in a package extension: load GeometricOptimizers and AbstractNeuralNetworks to make it available.

using VlasovMethods, GeometricOptimizers, AbstractNeuralNetworks, Random
m = 10
model = Chain(Dense(m, m, tanh), Dense(m, m, tanh), Dense(m, 1, identity; use_bias = false))
ps, losses = train_hnn(model, params(NeuralNetwork(model)), data, target;
    η = 1e-4, epochs = 1000, batchsize = 100, rng = Xoshiro(42))

model is the Hamiltonian H of the reduced field, a function of the reduced positions, and model(x, ps) its value at one sample. It is a Chain of Dense layers whose activations are tanh or identity, with one output. data holds one sample per column, the leading coefficients of the particle basis divided by the square root of their values of its spectrum, and target the matching accelerations, of the same size. The loss of a sample is $||\nabla_x H(x) - y||^2$: the gradient of the Hamiltonian with respect to its input against the acceleration. The step of an epoch descends the sum of that over the samples of its batch, and losses[j] is the sum over all n samples after the step of epoch j.

Epoch j forms the gradient of the loss over its batch with respect to the parameters, takes one GeometricOptimizers.optimization_step! of a TrainingOptimizer built with GradientMethod() and linesearch = η, and records the loss over all samples. batches holds one batch per epoch, as the indices of the samples that the epoch steps on, and an index may repeat in a batch. Without it, epoch j draws its batch with replacement, as rand(rng, 1:n, batchsize).

ps₀ is a NetworkParameters of model, and the trainer copies it: the returned parameters are a new set, and ps₀ holds the values it held at the call. data, target and ps₀ are of one element type, which the parameters and losses are returned in.

Throws

  • DimensionMismatch when data and target do not have the same size, when the first axis of data is not the input dimension of model, and when batches does not hold one batch per epoch.
  • ArgumentError when a layer of model is not a Dense layer of tanh or identity, when model maps to more than one output, when data holds no sample, when batchsize or epochs is below one, when η is not finite and positive, when a batch of batches is not of batchsize indices in 1:n, when the element types differ, and when a loss is not finite, which names the epoch.

build_grid_parity_factors(grid_x, grid_v, mx, mv) -> BilinearStiefelLayer

VlasovMethods.build_spline_parity_factors — Function
build_spline_parity_factors(basis_x, basis_v, mx, mv) -> BilinearStiefelLayer

Return the BilinearStiefelLayer of GeometricMachineLearning that encodes the coefficients of a tensor-product spline on basis_x ⊗ basis_v with $m_x$ spatial and $m_v$ velocity modes, with metric_factors = (R_x, R_v), $M_x = R_x^\mathsf{T} R_x$ and $M_v = R_v^\mathsf{T} R_v$ for the mass matrices of the two bases. basis_v is a clamped BSplineBasis: the mirror of its function $j$ is the function $N_v + 1 - j$.

Defined in a package extension: load GeometricMachineLearning to make it available.

$R_x$ is the upper Cholesky factor of $M_x$. $R_v$ is a square-root factor and not triangular: $R_v = S^\mathsf{T} (R^+ \oplus R^-) S$, with $S$ the orthogonal parity split of the layer (index $j$ with its mirror $N_v + 1 - j$, scaled by $1/\sqrt 2$) and $R^\pm$ the upper Cholesky factors of the even and odd blocks of $S M_v S^\mathsf{T}$. A triangular factor of the whole $M_v$ does not commute with the reflection $J$ of the velocity coefficients, so an even latent state would decode to a function that is not even; $R_v$ commutes with $J$, and the layer applies $R^+$ to the even half and $R^-$ to the odd half.

With $\Phi_x$ and $\Phi_v$ the Stiefel bases of the layer, the spatial modes $R_x^{-1} \Phi_x$ and the even and odd velocity modes $R_v^{-1} S_\pm^\mathsf{T} \Phi_v$ are orthonormal in $M_x$ and in $M_v$: the latent coordinates are $L^2$-orthonormal projections of the spline. They are not canonical coordinates of the Vlasov bracket: linearised at an equilibrium $f_0$, the bracket of the even and the odd coordinates is $-D_x \otimes K_v$, not the identity. dual_parity_modes gives odd modes for which it is the identity.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when the velocity domain is not symmetric, $v_{\min} = -v_{\max}$ to $10\,\mathrm{eps}(T)$ relative to $v_{\max}$, or the velocity knot vector is not symmetric under $v \mapsto -v$, and when basis_v has an odd number of functions. A clamped basis of degree $p$ on $N$ cells has $N + p$, so $N + p$ must be even.

VlasovMethods.dual_parity_modes — Function
dual_parity_modes(x_modes, even_v, odd_v, df0; nq = 24) -> Matrix

Return the coefficients of the odd modes $h = e B^{-1}$ dual to the even product modes $g_a = \varphi_i \psi^+_j$ for the Vlasov bracket linearised at the homogeneous equilibrium $f_0(v)$: with $Q_a = \langle g_a, \delta f\rangle$ and $P_b = \langle h_b, \delta f\rangle$, $\{Q_a, P_b\} = \delta_{ab}$ and $\{P_b, P_{b'}\} = 0$.

Defined in a package extension: load GeometricMachineLearning to make it available.

x_modes is a vector of Splines on one periodic basis, even_v and odd_v vectors of Splines on one velocity basis, even and odd in $v$, and df0 the derivative $f_0'(v)$, whose values are converted to the element type of x_modes. The odd product modes are $e_c = \varphi_k \psi^-_l$, and $B = -D_x \otimes K_v$ with $(D_x)_{ik} = \int \varphi_i' \varphi_k \,\mathrm{d}x$ and $(K_v)_{jl} = \int f_0'(v)\, \psi^+_j \psi^-_l \,\mathrm{d}v$. The product index runs over $x$ fastest, the order of vec(Q_red) in BilinearStiefelLayer. Column $b$ of the result is $h_b$, the coefficients on the tensor basis, again $x$ fastest. $K_v$ is assembled with nq Gauß–Legendre points per cell, enough for a Maxwellian on cells of unit width to round-off.

The dual modes are not $L^2$-orthonormal, so a frontend that encodes $P$ with them is not an isometry. Neither a monotone nor a positive $f_0$ is needed: the construction holds for the two-stream equilibrium $f_0 = v^2 M(v)$ as for the Maxwellian $M$.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when the splines of x_modes, of even_v or of odd_v do not share one basis, when the basis of x_modes is not periodic, when odd_v is on another basis than even_v, when even_v and odd_v differ in length, when an x mode is constant, when $D_x$ is singular because a $\cos mx$ lacks its $\sin mx$, and when $K_v$ is numerically singular, $\mathrm{cond}(K_v) > 1/(100\,\mathrm{eps}(T))$. Each message names its condition.

VlasovMethods.klimontovich_deposition — Function
klimontovich_deposition(basis_x, basis_v, weights) -> KlimontovichDepositionLayer

Return the deposition of weighted particles onto the tensor-product spline space basis_x ⊗ basis_v, a periodic basis in $x$ and a clamped one in $v$. Called with the particle positions x and velocities v, the layer returns the coefficient matrix

\[\tilde c = M_x^{-1}\, c\, M_v^{-1}, \qquad c_{ab} = \sum_{i} w_i\, \Lambda^x_a(x_i)\, \Lambda^v_b(v_i),\]

the coefficients of the $L^2$ projection $\Pi_h \mathbf{J}$ of the Klimontovich density $\mathbf{J} = \sum_i w_i \delta(x - x_i) \delta(v - v_i)$, not the moments $c$. A particle outside the velocity domain adds nothing.

Defined in a package extension: load GeometricMachineLearning to make it available.

Because $\Pi_h$ is self-adjoint, $\langle \Pi_h \mathbf{J}, g\rangle = \langle \mathbf{J}, \Pi_h g \rangle$ for every $g$. The Galerkin-truncated Lie–Poisson bracket on $\tilde c$ matches the particle bracket of two smooth observables up to the truncation error of the spline space, and it does not satisfy the Jacobi identity at a fixed mesh.

Throws

  • DimensionMismatch when x, v and weights differ in length.

VlasovMethods.kruskal_oberman_free_energy — Function
kruskal_oberman_free_energy(df0, grid_v, wavenumbers) -> KruskalObermanFreeEnergy

Return the Kruskal–Oberman free energy of the Vlasov–Poisson equations linearised at a monotone equilibrium $f_0(v)$, on the spatial Fourier modes $\cos kx$, $\sin kx$ of wavenumbers and the symmetric velocity grid grid_v.

Defined in a package extension: load GeometricMachineLearning to make it available.

A state is the matrix C of the values of $\delta f$ at the nodes grid_v, one row per spatial mode, in the order $\cos k_1 x, \sin k_1 x, \cos k_2 x, \dots$, each mode normalised in $L^2(0, 2\pi)$. The velocity space is nodal, with the grid width $\Delta v$ as the weight of each node, so the Galerkin matrix of a multiplication is diagonal. Called with C, the free energy returns (value, gradient, rhs): the value

\[H_{\mathrm{KO}} = \frac12 \sum_q \Delta v\, w_0(v_q) \lVert C_{:, q} \rVert^2 + \frac12 \sum_i \frac{\rho_i^2}{k_i^2}, \qquad w_0 = -\frac{v}{f_0'(v)}, \quad \rho_i = \sum_q \Delta v\, C_{iq},\]

its gradient with respect to C, and the Hamiltonian vector field

\[\partial_t \begin{pmatrix} \delta f^+ \\ \delta f^- \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \mathcal{A} \\ \mathcal{A} & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \delta H / \delta f^+ \\ \delta H / \delta f^- \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathcal{A} = f_0'(v)\, \partial_x ,\]

whose Galerkin matrix is $\mathrm{i}k K_v$ on the mode $k$, with $K_v = \mathrm{diag}(\Delta v\, f_0'(v_q))$. The operator is skew, so the free energy is conserved, and the vector field is the linearised Vlasov–Poisson right-hand side $-v\, \partial_x \delta f + f_0'(v)\, \partial_x \Phi$, $-\partial_x^2 \Phi = \rho$.

For a non-monotone $f_0$, such as the two-stream equilibrium, $w_0$ is not defined; the canonical coordinates of that case are those of dual_parity_modes.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when wavenumbers is empty, when a wavenumber is zero (the constant mode has no conjugate), when two wavenumbers have the same $|k|$ (they give the same modes), when grid_v is not symmetric or has an odd number of nodes, and when $v\, f_0'(v) \ge 0$ at a node, which names dual_parity_modes.
  • DimensionMismatch when C does not have one row per mode and one column per node.

VlasovMethods.tie_continuum_frontends! — Function
tie_continuum_frontends!((spline_layer, ps_spline), (grid_layer, ps_grid),
    (dlra_layer, ps_dlra), E_x, E_v) -> (ps_grid, ps_dlra)

Set the Stiefel bases of the grid and the low-rank frontends from those of the spline frontend, so that a phase-space function given as a spline, as its grid values or as a low-rank factorisation of them maps to the same latent state up to the quadrature error of the grid.

Defined in a package extension: load GeometricMachineLearning to make it available.

spline_layer is a layer of build_spline_parity_factors, grid_layer one of build_grid_parity_factors, and dlra_layer a LowRankStiefelLayer with the metric factors of grid_layer; ps_* are their parameters, and the function overwrites ps_grid and ps_dlra with the same bases. E_x and E_v are the nodal evaluation matrices, E_x[i, a] the value of the spatial basis function a at grid node i, and E_v the same for the velocity basis.

A spline mode with orthonormal coordinates $a$ is the function with coefficients $R^{-1} a$, and its grid coordinates are its nodal values times $\sqrt{w}$. The grid bases are the polar factors of these images, $\Phi_x^{\mathrm{grid}} = \mathrm{polar}(W_x^{1/2} E_x R_x^{-1} \Phi_x)$ and the even half of the same map in velocity; the polar factor keeps them orthonormal and differs from the image by the quadrature error of the Gram matrix.

The layers share one velocity basis $\Phi_v$ between the even and the odd coordinates. The velocity bases are tied through the even modes: the odd spline modes differ from the odd extension of the even ones near $v = 0$, because $R^+ \ne R^-$, so the odd coordinates of the three frontends agree only to that difference, which falls more slowly under refinement than the quadrature error of the even ones.

Throws

  • ArgumentError when dlra_layer does not carry the metric factors of grid_layer.
  • DimensionMismatch when E_x or E_v does not map the spline basis to the grid, and when a Stiefel basis of ps_grid or ps_dlra does not have the size of the basis it receives.
