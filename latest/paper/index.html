Reproducing the paper's examples

The reduced-order paper has two examples: the analytic quadratic model, whose solution has a closed form, and the self-consistent bump-on-tail problem. Four scripts in scripts/ compute both. This page says how to run them and what each one writes. It runs no script, and it shows no figure: the figures are files that the scripts write.

Running a script

The scripts have their own environment, scripts/Project.toml, which takes VlasovMethods from the checkout it is in. Instantiate it once, from the root of the repository:

julia --project=scripts -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'

Every script takes --runs-dir and --results-dir. A script writes its data, the HDF5 files, to the first, and its figures and tables to the second. The defaults are runs/ and results/ at the root of the repository, which git ignores. A flag that a script does not know stops it with an ArgumentError, which names the flags it takes.

julia --project=scripts scripts/bump_on_tail_training.jl --runs-dir /tmp/runs --results-dir /tmp/results

Each figure is a PNG named item<NN>_<what>.png, after the item of the paper that it draws. A figure plots the quantity of its item at the script's parameters. It does not reproduce the paper's numbers: the paper's draws ran on Julia 1.3.1, and no value is pinned against them. Every HDF5 file carries the root attributes commit, the commit of VlasovMethods that wrote it, and command, the script and its arguments.

The bump-on-tail problem

Three scripts run in this order, each with the same options, because the options set the name of the files that the next script reads. Each takes the options of the paper's training run: --dt (0.1), --final-time (25.0), --particles (10000), --cells of the spline potential (16), --degree of the spline (3) and --seed (1234). The particles are drawn once, at the reference wave number κ₀ = 0.3, and a sample at the wave number κ marches them on the same domain with the scale χ = κ/κ₀. Every default is the paper's value, so each script runs the paper's parameters without options.

bump_on_tail_training.jl

The training sweep: the full-order march of the ten wave numbers κ evenly spaced in [0.1, 0.5].

  • Paper command: julia --project=scripts scripts/bump_on_tail_training.jl
  • Measured run time: 15 s.
  • Writes: <run id>.h5 in the runs directory, the snapshots of the sweep with the accelerations of every saved state, and the integrator parameters in its group integrator, with the root attribute seed beside commit and command.

bump_on_tail_projections.jl

The reduced bases of the training sweep: the cotangent-lift basis of the particles, and the POD basis of the field. Each keeps the leading vectors that carry all but its tolerance of the energy of its spectrum: --particle-tolerance (1e-9) and --field-tolerance (1e-5).

  • Paper command: julia --project=scripts scripts/bump_on_tail_projections.jl --particle-tolerance 1e-9 --field-tolerance 1e-5
  • Measured run time: 34 s, with the bases of k = 205 particle vectors and kₑ = 570 field vectors.
  • Writes: in the runs directory, <run id>_projections.h5, the particle basis with the field basis in its group field, and <run id>_vectorfield.h5, the reduced positions X and accelerations A of every saved state, each of size (k, nₛ, nparam). Both carry the root attributes commit, command and seed. In the results directory, the two spectra: item11_particle_spectrum.png and item11_field_spectrum.png.

bump_on_tail_testing.jl

The test sweep: ten wave numbers drawn uniform in [0.1, 0.5] after the seed, each marched by the full-order model (HFM) and by two reduced models of the particle basis (ROM): one with the DEIM field of the field basis, which the reference's testing script marches, and one with the full field of the lifted particles, which the reference keeps commented out beside it. --samples (10) sets their number.

  • Paper command: julia --project=scripts scripts/bump_on_tail_testing.jl

  • Measured run time: 43 s.

  • Writes: in the results directory, growth_rates.csv, the growth rate of the field energy of each test sample, HFM, DEIM model and full-field model, under the header kappa,gamma_hfm,gamma_rom,gamma_rom_full, and the figures. A figure of one reduced model is the DEIM model's, and its twin that ends in _full is the full-field model's:

    itemfileswhat they plot
    24item24_<error>_time[_full].png, item24_<error>_kappa[_full].pngthe four trajectory errors x2, xmax, v2 and vmax against time for every sample, and against κ at T, 0.75T and 0.5T
    25item25_potential_error[_full].pngthe absolute error of the potential against time
    26item26_field_energy.png, item26_field_energy_best.png, item26_field_energy_worst.pngthe field energy of the three models, of every sample, and of the samples whose growth rates of HFM and DEIM model agree best and worst
    27item27_energy.png, item27_energy_drift.png, item27_energy_drift_kappa.pngthe energy and its relative drift of the three models, and the size of the drift against κ at T, 0.75T and 0.5T
    28item28_growth_rate.pngthe growth rate against κ of the three models
    29item29_particles_t0.png, item29_particles_T_hfm.png, item29_particles_T_rom[_full].pngthe particles of the sample nearest κ₀, at t = 0 and, for each model, at T

    The growth rate is half the slope of the least-squares line of log W through the first four maxima of the field energy W, or through all of them when there are fewer, with a window of 5 saved states on each side of a maximum. A sample with fewer than two maxima has no rate, NaN, and item 26 ranks only the samples that have one. The errors and the field energy are drawn on log axes, as the reference draws them; the errors from the first save on, because at t = 0 they are those of the projection alone.

The analytic quadratic model

analytic_quadratic.jl

The model H(q, p) = ½|p|² + ½β²|q|² of 1000 particles, trained on the closed form of six β in [5.95, 6.05] and tested at β = 6. The full model is the closed form on the step 0.001. The two reduced forms march the projected initial condition on the step 0.0001: the ODE form in the POD basis of k = 5 with RK4, and the PODE form in the cotangent-lift basis of k = 20 with Lobatto IIIA–IIIB(2). --final-time and --saves set the final time and the number of saved states after t = 0, and --particles (1000) the number of particles.

  • Paper command: julia --project=scripts scripts/analytic_quadratic.jl --final-time 1000 --saves 10000. The default is --final-time 100 --saves 1000, a tenth of the paper's steps.
  • Measured run time: 135 s, with the defaults.
  • Writes: in the runs directory, analytic_full.h5, analytic_ode_k5.h5 and analytic_pode_k20.h5, each with the saved times t, the positions q, the velocities p and the Hamiltonian H of every saved state, and the root attributes dt and nsave; the two reduced files also hold the reduced states z. In the results directory, the two spectra of the training set, item11_analytic_pod_spectrum.png and item11_analytic_cotangent_spectrum.png; the errors of both reduced forms against the closed form, item24_analytic_x.png and item24_analytic_v.png; and their energy drift, item27_analytic_energy_drift.png. A reduced state that is not finite stops the run with an error.

How the run times were measured

Each time is one run of the script with its defaults, in a new Julia process, after Pkg.instantiate() and Pkg.precompile() of the scripts environment: Julia 1.13.2 on an Apple silicon Mac, with other jobs running beside it. The time includes the load of the packages and the compilation of the script's own calls.
